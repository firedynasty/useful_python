## 4-Out 1-In Offense: Plays and Concepts for 4 Perimeter, 1 Post Player

The 4-out 1-in offense is a widely used basketball system where four players are spaced around the perimeter and one player operates as a post presence, typically near the low block or high post. This setup is praised for its spacing, driving lanes, and versatility, and is effective at all levels of basketball[1][2][3][4].

### **Key Concepts and Actions**

- **Spacing:** Four players are positioned outside the three-point line—usually two in the slots (just above the free-throw line extended) and two on the wings—with the post player inside, often on the weak side to keep the lane open for drives and cuts[1][5][3].
- **Post Player Role:** The post player (often the “5”) can stay on the weak side block, flash to the high post, or set screens for perimeter players. Their main jobs are to be available for dump-down passes, clean up offensive rebounds, and serve as a scoring option if they have a mismatch[1][6].
- **Perimeter Movement:** The four outside players engage in ball movement, drive-and-kick actions, basket cuts, and off-ball screens to create scoring opportunities and shift the defense[2][4].
- **Ball Screens:** The post player can set ball screens for perimeter players, leading to pick-and-roll or pick-and-pop situations[2][3].
- **Cutting and Screening:** After passing, perimeter players often cut to the basket or set screens for each other, keeping the defense moving and creating open looks[2][4].

### **Sample Play: 4-Out 1-High**

A specific play designed for a 4-out 1-in setup with the post at the high post (free-throw line) is as follows[7]:

- Set up with two wings at the free-throw line extended, two guards at the top, and the post player at the nail (center of the free-throw line).
- Guard 1 dribbles to a side and looks for a backdoor cut from 4.
- If the backdoor isn’t open, 1 passes and uses a screen from 5 (the post), then basket cuts looking for a lob.
- The play continues with a series of cuts, screens, and ball reversals, always keeping the post player involved as a screener and potential scoring option.
- The post player can also set a down screen for a shooter coming to the top, or roll to the block for a post-up opportunity[7].

### **Villanova’s 4-Out 1-In Motion Offense**

Jay Wright’s Villanova teams are a modern example of 4-out 1-in success. Their version involves:

- 4 perimeter players filling the two slots and two wings, with the post player in the weak side “dunker spot” or mid-post.
- Perimeter players pass, cut, and fill, while the post player shifts with the ball to maintain spacing and be available for dump-downs or offensive rebounds.
- Ball screens and weak side exchanges are used to remove help defenders and create driving lanes[8][3].
