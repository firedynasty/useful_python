Chapter 24: Paul Before Felix; Tertullus's Accusation; Paul's Defense; Felix Trembles
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's trial before the Roman governor - exposing the flattery and falsehood of his accusers while Paul gives a model defense combining legal skill with spiritual testimony.

Key theological themes:
1. **Tertullus's Flattery**: The hired orator opened with shameless compliments to Felix - a corrupt governor who "allowed himself in all manner of wickedness." "Such flattering compliments as Tertullus soothed him up with" revealed the prosecutors' weakness.

2. **The Pestilent Fellow**: Paul was called "a pestilent fellow, and a mover of sedition among all the Jews throughout the world, and a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes." They dressed him "in the skins of wild beasts" to justify their treatment.

3. **Neither Can They Prove**: Paul answered calmly, denying he had disputed or raised up the people anywhere: "Neither can they prove the things whereof they now accuse me." Innocence combined with humility.

4. **After the Way They Call Heresy**: "This I confess unto thee, that after the way which they call heresy, so worship I the God of my fathers." True Christianity has been called heresy in every age - "never the worse for its being put into an ill name."

5. **Believing All Things Written**: Paul believed "all things which are written in the law and in the prophets." His religion was grounded on scripture entire and pure - not tradition, church authority, or human reason alone.

6. **Hope of the Resurrection**: "I have hope toward God... that there shall be a resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust." This hope governed all his worship and practice - aiming at heaven, not worldly advantage.

7. **A Conscience Void of Offence**: "Herein do I exercise myself, to have always a conscience void of offence toward God, and toward men." He worked at keeping his conscience clear - it required effort and discipline.

8. **Felix Trembled**: Paul "reasoned of righteousness, temperance, and judgment to come" before Felix and his wife Drusilla. "Felix trembled" - yet answered, "Go thy way for this time; when I have a convenient season, I will call for thee."

9. **Hoping for a Bribe**: "He hoped also that money should have been given him of Paul" - so he sent for him often. The corrupt judge prolonged the imprisonment for two years, "willing to show the Jews a pleasure."

Practical applications:
- Flattery often accompanies weak accusations
- True religion will be called heresy by its enemies
- Scripture alone is the rule of faith and practice
- Hope of resurrection governs the whole Christian life
- Trembling is not repenting; many are moved but not converted
- Convenient seasons never arrive for those who delay
