Chapter 22: Paul's Defense Before the People; His Conversion Retold; Roman Citizenship
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's testimony before the hostile crowd - recounting his Jewish credentials, his persecution of Christians, and his dramatic conversion - demonstrating how personal testimony can be both defense and witness.

Key theological themes:
1. **Composure Under Trial**: Paul addressed the mob "with a good courage" and "a good conscience." "None of these things move me" - neither the tumult nor his bonds shook his settled spirit.

2. **Speaking Hebrew**: "When they heard that he spake in the Hebrew tongue to them, they kept the more silence." He spoke to be understood, showing continued respect for his countrymen despite their abuse.

3. **A Pharisee's Son**: "I am verily a man which am a Jew... brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, and taught according to the perfect manner of the law of the fathers." He was "zealous toward God, as ye all are this day."

4. **I Persecuted This Way**: "I persecuted this way unto the death, binding and delivering into prisons both men and women." His conversion was not from favorable prepossession but despite violent opposition - proving divine power.

5. **The Light from Heaven**: "Suddenly there shined round about me a great light from heaven." Christ asked, "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?" - revealing that persecuting saints is persecuting Christ himself.

6. **Lord, What Wilt Thou Have Me Do?**: Paul's immediate surrender: "Lord, what shall I do?" The voice directed him to Damascus where "it shall be told thee of all things which are appointed for thee to do."

7. **Ananias's Testimony**: "A devout man according to the law, having a good report of all the Jews" - not likely to instill anti-Jewish sentiment. He declared: "The God of our fathers hath chosen thee, that thou shouldest know his will, and see that Just One."

8. **I Will Send Thee to the Gentiles**: Christ appeared to Paul praying in the temple: "Depart: for I will send thee far hence unto the Gentiles." At this word the mob erupted in fury - showing their hatred was about Gentile inclusion.

9. **Roman Citizenship**: About to be scourged, Paul asked, "Is it lawful for you to scourge a man that is a Roman, and uncondemned?" The chief captain was alarmed, "for he had bound him."

Practical applications:
- Personal testimony is powerful evidence of grace
- Conversion must be attributed to divine power, not human persuasion
- God often gives gracious answers different from what we pray for
- Christ's call to the Gentiles offended Jewish pride then as now
- Lawful protections may rightly be used by Christ's servants
