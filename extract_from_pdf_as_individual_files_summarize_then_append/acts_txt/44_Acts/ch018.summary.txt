Chapter 18: Paul at Corinth; Gallio's Indifference; Apollos
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's extended ministry at Corinth, Christ's encouraging vision, and the introduction of the eloquent Apollos - showing both divine protection and human partnership in gospel work.

Key theological themes:
1. **Tentmaking Ministry**: Paul "wrought" with Aquila and Priscilla, being "of the same craft." Though entitled to support, he worked with his hands. "See how humble Paul was, and wonder that so great a man could stoop so low." Jews taught their children trades: "He that teaches not his son a trade is as if he taught him to be a thief."

2. **Pressed in Spirit**: When Silas and Timothy arrived, "Paul was pressed in the spirit" - more intent than ever, "the love of Christ constrained him." He testified that Jesus was Christ with all possible solemnity and seriousness.

3. **Your Blood Upon Your Own Heads**: When Jews opposed and blasphemed, Paul "shook his raiment" declaring himself clean from their blood. "If they perish in their unbelief their blood is not to be required at his hands." He turned to the Gentiles without abandoning his work.

4. **Christ's Vision**: "Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace: For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city." Christ knew his elect before they knew him - "I have them, for I am sure to have them."

5. **Much People in Corinth**: Even in that profane city, "full of impurity," Christ had many people. "In this heap that seems to be all chaff, there is wheat; in this ore that seems to be all dross, there is gold. Let us not despair concerning any place."

6. **Eighteen Months Ministry**: Paul continued a year and six months "teaching the word of God among them." Converts need teaching - "Those that are converted have still need to be taught the word of God."

7. **Gallio's Indifference**: The Roman deputy dismissed the Jews' accusations as "a question of words and names." Though rightly refusing to persecute, he wrongly despised divine matters as beneath him. When Sosthenes was beaten before him, "Gallio cared for none of those things" - a warning against religious indifference.

8. **Apollos Instructed**: "An eloquent man, and mighty in the scriptures," yet knowing only John's baptism. Aquila and Priscilla "expounded unto him the way of God more perfectly." Great gifts need proper instruction; private Christians may teach public ministers.

Practical applications:
- Ministers may support themselves without shame
- Christ's promise of presence enables bold witness
- No place is too wicked for God to have his people
- Religious indifference is as dangerous as active persecution
- The learned can learn from the humble
