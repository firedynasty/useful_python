Chapter 3: Healing of the Lame Man and Peter's Sermon
----------------------------------------
Henry presents this miracle as opening the door for gospel preaching, with the sermon both convicting of sin and offering mercy through Christ.

Key theological themes:
1. **The Setting**: Peter and John went to the temple at the hour of prayer (3 PM). Public worship brings believers together where Christ may be proclaimed. The lame beggar at the Beautiful Gate represents spiritual lameness - "without strength, lame from our birth, unable to work or walk in God's service."

2. **The Miracle**: When asked for alms, Peter said "Silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk." This demonstrates that Christ's servants, though poor in worldly goods, are rich in spiritual gifts. The cure was immediate and complete - the man walked, leaped, and praised God.

3. **The Name of Jesus**: Peter credited the cure entirely to Christ's name and power. Those who "have been with Jesus" should be evident by their character and conduct. The miracle confirmed their commission from the risen Christ.

4. **Peter's Sermon**: He disclaimed personal power or holiness, transferring all glory to Christ. God has "glorified his Son Jesus" whom they delivered up, denied before Pilate, and killed - yet God raised him. "You killed the Prince of life" - a powerful indictment showing the contrast between their murderous intent and Christ's life-giving purpose.

5. **The Call to Repentance**: Despite their guilt, Henry notes Peter's pastoral care - calling them "brethren" and attributing their sin partly to ignorance. The call: "Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out." Repentance and conversion are distinct - repentance changes the mind, conversion changes the direction.

6. **Christ the Prophet Like Moses**: Peter quotes Deuteronomy 18:15 - Christ is the prophet God promised to raise up. All must hear him, for "every soul which will not hear that prophet shall be destroyed."

7. **The Covenant with Abraham**: The Jews were "children of the prophets and of the covenant." Christ was sent "to you first...to bless you, in turning away every one of you from his iniquities." The great blessing is deliverance from sin.

Practical applications:
- Give what you have, even if not silver and gold
- All spiritual benefit comes through Christ's name and power
- Conviction should lead to repentance, not despair
- God's blessing turns us from our iniquities
