Chapter 16: Timothy; The Macedonian Call; Lydia; The Philippian Jailer
----------------------------------------
Henry follows Paul's second missionary journey - adopting Timothy, receiving the Macedonian vision, and planting the church at Philippi through notable conversions.

Key theological themes:
1. **Timothy Chosen**: A disciple "well reported of by the brethren," son of a believing Jewish mother and Greek father. Paul circumcised him "because of the Jews which were in those quarters" - not as necessary to salvation, but "when it was conducive to edification."

2. **Forbidden and Directed**: "Forbidden of the Holy Ghost to preach the word in Asia" and not suffered to go into Bithynia. "The Spirit of Jesus suffered them not" - Christ's servants must be "under the check and conduct of the Spirit."

3. **The Macedonian Vision**: "There stood a man of Macedonia, and prayed him, saying, Come over into Macedonia, and help us." Paul "immediately endeavoured to go" - "God's calls must be complied with immediately."

4. **Philippi**: The chief city, a Roman colony. Despite the supernatural call, they had "cold entertainment" at first - "abiding certain days" before any took notice.

5. **The Women's Prayer Meeting**: On the sabbath they found "where prayer was wont to be made" by a river, and "spoke unto the women which resorted thither." Though they had a grand call, their first audience was humble.

6. **Lydia's Conversion**: "Whose heart the Lord opened, that she attended unto the things which were spoken of Paul." Conversion-work is God's work, heart-work. "An unconverted soul is shut up... when the heart is opened, the ear is opened to his word, the lips opened in prayer, the hand opened in charity."

7. **The Spirit of Divination Cast Out**: A damsel possessed "brought her masters much gain by soothsaying." Paul commanded the spirit to come out "in the name of Jesus Christ." Her masters, seeing their profit gone, dragged Paul and Silas before the magistrates.

8. **Imprisonment and Earthquake**: Beaten and imprisoned, "at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God." An earthquake opened the doors. The jailer, about to kill himself, was stopped by Paul.

9. **The Jailer's Conversion**: "What must I do to be saved?" The answer: "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house." He was baptized with his household "the same hour of the night."

Practical applications:
- God guides by closing doors as well as opening them
- The Lord must open hearts for the word to take effect
- Suffering can be the occasion for singing and witnessing
- Salvation is offered to households, not just individuals
