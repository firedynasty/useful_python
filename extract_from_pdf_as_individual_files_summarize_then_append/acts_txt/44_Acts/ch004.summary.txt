Chapter 4: Peter and John Before the Sanhedrin
----------------------------------------
Henry shows the first organized opposition to the apostles, yet their boldness through the Spirit and the church's united prayer demonstrate the unstoppable advance of the gospel.

Key theological themes:
1. **Opposition Arises**: The priests, Sadducees, and temple captain were "grieved" at the apostles' preaching. The Sadducees denied resurrection; the priests resented Christ being preached. Yet despite arrest, the word prevailed - believers grew to 5,000. "The church's suffering days have been her growing days."

2. **The Trial**: Before Annas, Caiaphas, and the Sanhedrin, Peter was "filled with the Holy Ghost" - fulfilling Christ's promise of words given in that hour. He boldly testified that the lame man was healed "by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead."

3. **The Stone Rejected**: Quoting Psalm 118:22, Peter declared Christ "the stone which was set at nought of you builders, which is become the head of the corner." The religious leaders who should have recognized the Messiah rejected him.

4. **Exclusive Salvation**: "Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other name under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved" (v.12). Henry emphasizes: salvation is our chief concern; we cannot save ourselves; many names pretend to save but cannot; only Christ's name gives access to God and acceptance with Him.

5. **The Council's Dilemma**: They could not deny the miracle, could not answer the apostles' arguments, and dared not punish them for fear of the people. They only threatened them to "speak henceforth to no man in this name."

6. **Courageous Response**: "Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye. For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard." When human commands contradict divine commands, God must be obeyed.

7. **The Church's Prayer**: Returning to their company, they prayed together, acknowledging God as Creator and quoting Psalm 2 about rulers gathering against the Lord's anointed. They asked not for safety but for boldness: "Grant unto thy servants, that with all boldness they may speak thy word."

8. **God's Answer**: The place was shaken, they were filled with the Holy Ghost, and spoke with boldness. The primitive church flourished - "of one heart and one soul," sharing possessions, with "great grace upon them all." Barnabas exemplified this generosity.

Practical applications:
- Opposition cannot stop the gospel
- Obey God rather than men when commands conflict
- Pray for boldness, not ease
- Christian unity involves sharing both spiritual and material blessings
