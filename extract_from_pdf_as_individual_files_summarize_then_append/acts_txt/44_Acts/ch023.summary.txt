Chapter 23: Before the Sanhedrin; Pharisees and Sadducees Divided; The Conspiracy; Paul Sent to Caesarea
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's shrewd defense before the council, his divine encouragement, and his narrow escape from assassination - showing both human wisdom and divine protection working together.

Key theological themes:
1. **A Good Conscience**: Paul declared, "I have lived in all good conscience before God until this day." He had aimed at nothing but to please God - "the testimony of his conscience" was his rejoicing.

2. **God Shall Smite Thee**: When the high priest ordered him struck, Paul responded: "God shall smite thee, thou whited wall: for sittest thou to judge me after the law, and commandest me to be smitten contrary to the law?" A prophetic denunciation, not sinful passion.

3. **Respect for Office**: Told he had reviled the high priest, Paul said, "I wist not, brethren, that he was the high priest: for it is written, Thou shalt not speak evil of the ruler of thy people." He honored the office even when the man failed it.

4. **Dividing the Council**: Perceiving the council was part Pharisees and part Sadducees, Paul cried, "I am a Pharisee, the son of a Pharisee: of the hope and resurrection of the dead I am called in question." This set them against each other.

5. **Sadducees and Pharisees**: "The Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, neither angel, nor spirit: but the Pharisees confess both." Paul aligned with the Pharisees on fundamental doctrine despite other differences.

6. **No Evil in This Man**: The Pharisees declared, "We find no evil in this man: but if a spirit or an angel hath spoken to him, let us not fight against God." Even adversaries bore witness to his innocence.

7. **The Lord Stood By Him**: That night Christ appeared: "Be of good cheer, Paul: for as thou hast testified of me in Jerusalem, so must thou bear witness also at Rome." Divine comfort when human friends failed to visit.

8. **The Forty Conspirators**: More than forty men bound themselves under a curse "that they would neither eat nor drink till they had killed Paul." They enlisted the chief priests to ambush him.

9. **Paul's Nephew**: The son of Paul's sister heard of the plot, told Paul, who sent him to the chief captain. Providence used ordinary means - a young man's alertness - to preserve his servant.

10. **Sent to Felix**: The captain sent Paul by night with 470 soldiers to Caesarea, with a letter explaining the case. "Thus Paul was brought off from his enemies, not by miracle, but by common providence."

Practical applications:
- A good conscience gives confidence before any tribunal
- Offices deserve respect even when officers fail
- Honest policy may be used in self-preservation
- Christ's presence comforts when human friends are absent
- Providence uses ordinary means for extraordinary deliverances
