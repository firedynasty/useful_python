Chapter 15: The Jerusalem Council
----------------------------------------
Henry presents the first great church council, settling whether Gentile converts must keep the law of Moses - a controversy threatening to divide the young church.

Key theological themes:
1. **The Controversy**: Certain men from Judea taught, "Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved." Henry warns against those who say, "Unless you believe just as we would have you believe... you cannot be saved."

2. **Paul's Opposition**: Paul and Barnabas had "no small dissension and disputation with them." As faithful servants of Christ, "they would not see his truths betrayed"; as spiritual fathers, "they would not see their liberties encroached upon."

3. **The Council Assembled**: "The apostles and elders came together for to consider of this matter." They did not give judgment rashly but heard both sides. "In the multitude of counsellors there is safety."

4. **Peter's Testimony**: God "made choice among us, that the Gentiles by my mouth should hear the word of the gospel, and believe." God "put no difference between us and them, purifying their hearts by faith."

5. **The Unbearable Yoke**: "Why tempt ye God, to put a yoke upon the neck of the disciples, which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?" The ceremonial law was heavy; Christ came to ease us of it.

6. **Salvation by Grace**: "We believe that through the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ we shall be saved, even as they." Jews and Gentiles saved the same way - by grace through faith, not by works of the law.

7. **James's Verdict**: "My sentence is, that we trouble not them, which from among the Gentiles are turned to God" - only that they abstain from pollution of idols, fornication, things strangled, and blood.

8. **The Letter Sent**: The council wrote to Gentile believers: "It seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things."

9. **Paul and Barnabas Separate**: A "sharp contention" arose over John Mark. They parted - Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus, Paul took Silas through Syria and Cilicia.

Practical applications:
- Christian liberty must be preserved against legalism
- Church disputes require careful, prayerful deliberation
- Unity in essentials, liberty in non-essentials
- Even godly men may disagree; God can use the separation
