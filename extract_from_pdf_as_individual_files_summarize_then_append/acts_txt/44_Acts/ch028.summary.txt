Chapter 28: Malta; The Viper; Arrival at Rome; Paul's Ministry There
----------------------------------------
Henry concludes Acts with Paul's ministry on Malta, his arrival in Rome, and his two-year witness there - showing that nothing can hinder the progress of the gospel, not even chains.

Key theological themes:
1. **Barbarous Kindness**: "The barbarous people showed us no little kindness: for they kindled a fire, and received us every one." God makes even strangers to be friends when his people are in need.

2. **Paul Gathering Sticks**: Though an apostle, "Paul was as busy as any of them in gathering sticks." He "would stoop to any thing by which he might be serviceable." We should reckon nothing below us but sin.

3. **The Viper's Attack**: "There came a viper out of the heat, and fastened on his hand." The people concluded, "No doubt this man is a murderer, whom, though he hath escaped the sea, yet vengeance suffereth not to live."

4. **He Felt No Harm**: Paul "shook off the beast into the fire, and felt no harm." The promise was fulfilled: "They shall take up serpents; and... it shall not hurt them." The people then said "he was a god."

5. **Healing on Malta**: Paul healed the father of Publius and "others also, which had diseases in the island." "A good man will endeavour to do good wherever the providence of God casts him."

6. **Christians at Rome**: At Puteoli and on the way to Rome, Paul "found brethren." When he saw them, "he thanked God, and took courage." Christian fellowship strengthens the weary traveler.

7. **Dwelling by Himself**: At Rome, "Paul was suffered to dwell by himself with a soldier that kept him." Even as a prisoner, God gave him liberty to receive visitors and preach.

8. **For the Hope of Israel**: To the Jewish leaders Paul declared: "For this cause therefore have I called for you... because that for the hope of Israel I am bound with this chain."

9. **Some Believed, Some Believed Not**: After his full exposition of Scripture, "some believed the things which were spoken, and some believed not." The gospel always divides.

10. **Isaiah's Prophecy Fulfilled**: Paul applied Isaiah 6 to their unbelief: "The heart of this people is waxed gross... lest they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears."

11. **Two Whole Years**: "Paul dwelt two whole years in his own hired house, and received all that came in unto him, preaching the kingdom of God, and teaching those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ, with all confidence, no man forbidding him."

Practical applications:
- God's people should do good wherever providence places them
- Natural religion knows there is divine justice, though imperfectly
- Christian fellowship is essential for encouragement in trials
- The gospel must be preached whether hearers receive it or not
- Imprisonment cannot silence the word of God
- Acts ends open-ended because the church's story continues
