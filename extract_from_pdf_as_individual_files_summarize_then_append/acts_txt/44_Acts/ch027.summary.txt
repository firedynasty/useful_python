Chapter 27: The Voyage to Rome; The Storm; The Shipwreck
----------------------------------------
Henry presents the dramatic sea voyage to Rome - a detailed narrative of storm, despair, divine assurance, and miraculous preservation that displays God's faithfulness to his promises.

Key theological themes:
1. **Julius's Courtesy**: The centurion "courteously entreated Paul, and gave him liberty to go unto his friends to refresh himself." God can put it into rulers' hearts to befriend his servants.

2. **Sailing Dangerous**: Paul warned that "this voyage will be with hurt and much damage, not only of the lading and ship, but also of our lives." But "the centurion believed the master and the owner of the ship, more than those things which were spoken by Paul."

3. **The South Wind Softly**: They set out with favorable weather, "supposing that they had obtained their purpose." But "not long after there arose against it a tempestuous wind, called Euroclydon." Fair beginnings do not guarantee fair endings.

4. **All Hope Taken Away**: After many days, "neither sun nor stars in many days appeared, and no small tempest lay on us, all hope that we should be saved was then taken away." They were at their wits' end.

5. **The Angel's Message**: Paul stood forth: "There stood by me this night the angel of God, whose I am, and whom I serve, saying, Fear not, Paul; thou must be brought before Caesar: and, lo, God hath given thee all them that sail with thee."

6. **I Believe God**: Paul's triumphant declaration: "Wherefore, sirs, be of good cheer: for I believe God, that it shall be even as it was told me." Divine promises are the ground of true confidence.

7. **Keeping the Sailors**: When the sailors plotted to escape in the boat, Paul told the centurion, "Except these abide in the ship, ye cannot be saved." God's promises do not exclude the use of means.

8. **Breaking Bread in Crisis**: Paul "took bread, and gave thanks to God in presence of them all." Even in extremity, the child of God acknowledges God at his table and encourages others.

9. **All Escaped Safe**: The ship ran aground and broke up, but "they escaped all safe to land" - all 276 souls, exactly as God had promised. Not one was lost.

Practical applications:
- Worldly wisdom often rejects divine counsel to its cost
- Stormy providences test but cannot overthrow God's promises
- Faith in God's word sustains hope when all else fails
- Means must be used even when outcomes are promised
- God is faithful to preserve all who belong to him
