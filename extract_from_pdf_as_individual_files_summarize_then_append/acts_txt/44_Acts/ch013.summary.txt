Chapter 13: Barnabas and Saul Sent Out; Paul's Sermon at Antioch
----------------------------------------
Henry presents the beginning of the formal mission to the Gentiles, with the church at Antioch sending out Barnabas and Saul under the Holy Spirit's direction.

Key theological themes:
1. **The Church at Antioch**: Well-furnished with "prophets and teachers" including Barnabas, Simeon, Lucius, Manaen (who had been raised with Herod the tetrarch), and Saul. They "ministered to the Lord, and fasted" - diligent faithful teachers truly minister unto the Lord.

2. **The Spirit's Call**: "The Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them." Christ by his Spirit has the nomination of his ministers. They are separated "to work; Christ keeps no servants to be idle."

3. **Ordination by Fasting and Prayer**: They "fasted and prayed, and laid their hands on them, and sent them away." This was not ordination to ministry in general, but to a particular service - the great work of spreading the gospel among the Gentiles.

4. **Elymas the Sorcerer**: At Paphos, the deputy Sergius Paulus desired to hear the gospel, but Elymas sought to turn him from the faith. Paul, "filled with the Holy Ghost," denounced him: "O full of all subtilty and all mischief, thou child of the devil, thou enemy of all righteousness." Elymas was struck blind - "a suitable punishment; he sought to blind the deputy, and is himself struck blind."

5. **Saul Called Paul**: Here Saul is first called Paul - his Roman name for ministry among Gentiles. The deputy believed, "astonished at the doctrine of the Lord."

6. **Paul's Sermon at Antioch in Pisidia**: A model of apostolic preaching to Jews - surveying God's dealings with Israel from Egypt through David to Christ. "Of this man's seed hath God according to his promise raised unto Israel a Saviour, Jesus."

7. **Justification by Faith**: "Through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins: And by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses."

8. **Gentiles Invited**: When Jews rejected the message, Paul declared, "Lo, we turn to the Gentiles." The Gentiles "were glad, and glorified the word of the Lord."

Practical applications:
- Ministers are separated to work, not to ease
- God's providence opens and closes doors for ministry
- Justification comes by faith in Christ, not by the law
- Rejection by some becomes opportunity for others
