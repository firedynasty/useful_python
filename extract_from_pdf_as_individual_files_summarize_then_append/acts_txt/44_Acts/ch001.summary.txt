Chapter 1: Christ's Ascension and Matthias Elected
----------------------------------------
Henry opens Acts by noting Luke's dedication to Theophilus and the continuity between his Gospel and this book. The apostles were commissioned to be witnesses of Christ's resurrection, empowered by the Holy Spirit.

Key theological themes:
1. **The Promise of the Spirit**: Christ commanded the disciples to wait in Jerusalem for the "baptism of the Holy Ghost." This was the Father's promise - spiritual blessings given by promise, not by nature, making them gifts of peculiar grace received by faith.

2. **The Limitation of Knowledge**: When the disciples asked about restoring the kingdom to Israel, Christ rebuked their curiosity about times and seasons. Henry emphasizes that God reserves certain knowledge for Himself, and believers should be content with sufficient instruction for duty rather than forbidden knowledge.

3. **The Nature of Christ's Witnesses**: The apostles were commissioned to be "witnesses unto me" - both proclaiming Christ as King and confirming their testimony through miracles and martyrdom. Their influence would extend from Jerusalem to the uttermost parts of the earth.

4. **The Ascension**: Christ ascended visibly while blessing them, received by a cloud (symbol of divine presence). The angels' message assured them of Christ's return "in like manner."

5. **United Prayer**: The 120 disciples continued "with one accord in prayer," demonstrating that unity and prayer prepare hearts for the Spirit's coming. "Where brethren dwell together in unity, there the Lord commands his blessing."

6. **Matthias Replaces Judas**: Peter's proposal to fill Judas's vacancy fulfilled Psalm 109:8. The qualifications required a constant companion of Christ's ministry. The lot (a lawful appeal to God in prayer) fell on Matthias, completing the twelve.

Practical applications:
- Christ's choice brings charge, not merely privilege
- We must not be curious about what God has not revealed
- Prayer and unity prepare us for spiritual blessing
- God's purposes are not frustrated by human failure
