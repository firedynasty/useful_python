Chapter 17: Thessalonica, Berea, and Athens
----------------------------------------
Henry traces the gospel from Jewish opposition in Macedonia to philosophical curiosity in Athens - showing that the same message meets different receptions in different soils.

Key theological themes:
1. **Ministry at Thessalonica**: Paul "as his manner was" went to the synagogue and "reasoned with them out of the scriptures" for three sabbath days. "The preaching of the gospel should be both scriptural preaching and rational." He opened and alleged that Christ must needs have suffered and risen.

2. **Opposition and Accusation**: Unbelieving Jews "moved with envy" gathered "lewd fellows of the baser sort" and set the city in uproar. They charged: "These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also" - saying "there is another king, one Jesus."

3. **World Turned Upside Down**: Henry notes this is true in one sense - the gospel gives "such a wide change to the stream, so directly contrary to what it was." The love of the world is rooted out, the way of the world contradicted. "It was only the kingdom of the devil in the world that they thus overturned."

4. **The Noble Bereans**: "These were more noble than those in Thessalonica" - they received the word with all readiness of mind and "searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so." True nobility is being disposed to receive truth, studying scriptures daily, making them our "oracle and touchstone."

5. **Daily Scripture Study**: "Searching the scriptures must be our daily work." Those who heard on the sabbath searched every day in the week - improving what they heard and preparing for what they would hear. "Those are truly noble that make the scriptures their oracle."

6. **Athens Full of Idols**: "Wholly given to idolatry" - more idols than all Greece besides. "Where human learning most flourished, idolatry most abounded." The greatest pretenders to reason were the greatest slaves to idols. "The world by wisdom knew not God."

7. **Paul's Spirit Stirred**: Seeing the city's idolatry, "his spirit was stirred in him." He did not admire their architecture or learning, but set about to correct their disorders in religion and turn them from idols to the living God.

8. **Mars Hill Discourse**: Paul preached natural religion to lead them toward revealed religion - the unknown God, the Creator who needs no temples, who "hath made of one blood all nations of men." He called them to repentance and declared the resurrection.

Practical applications:
- Scripture must be searched, not just heard
- Noble minds receive truth with readiness and test it by Scripture
- Human wisdom without revelation leads to idolatry
- Different audiences require different approaches to the same gospel
