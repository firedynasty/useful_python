Chapter 11: Peter's Defense; The Gospel at Antioch
----------------------------------------
Henry shows the church processing the inclusion of Gentiles, Peter's vindication before critics, and the expansion of the gospel to Antioch where disciples were first called "Christians."

Key theological themes:
1. **Criticism of Peter**: Those "of the circumcision contended with him" for eating with Gentiles. Even the apostle was held accountable by the church - "so far were they from looking upon him as infallible, or as the supreme head of the church."

2. **Peter's Defense**: He rehearsed the matter "from the beginning and expounded it by order." He explained his vision, the Spirit's direction, Cornelius's vision, and the Spirit falling on Gentiles. His conclusion: "What was I, that I could withstand God?"

3. **The Church Satisfied**: "When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life." They turned censure into praise.

4. **Repentance unto Life**: Henry emphasizes: repentance is unto life - both spiritual life now and eternal life forever. It is God's gift, not man's work. "Where God designs to give life he gives repentance."

5. **The Gospel at Antioch**: Those scattered by persecution "went every where preaching the word." At Antioch, "the hand of the Lord was with them: and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord." Persecution advanced what it meant to suppress.

6. **Barnabas's Ministry**: Sent from Jerusalem to Antioch, he "saw the grace of God" and "was glad." He was "a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith." He exhorted them "with purpose of heart to cleave unto the Lord."

7. **Christians First Named**: At Antioch "the disciples were first called Christians" - taking their name from Christ's office as the Anointed One. This superseded reproachful names, united Jew and Gentile under one title, and bound them to live worthy of the name.

8. **Famine Relief**: Agabus prophesied a coming famine. The disciples "determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judea" - the first recorded inter-church charitable collection.

Practical applications:
- Be slow to censure, quick to understand others' actions
- God's purposes triumph through opposition
- Good names carry obligations to holy living
- Prosperity should prompt charity, not hoarding
