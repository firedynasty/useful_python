Chapter 5: Ananias and Sapphira; Apostles Arrested
----------------------------------------
Henry presents this chapter as showing both divine judgment against hypocrisy and divine power supporting the apostles through persecution.

Key theological themes:
1. **The Sin of Ananias and Sapphira**: They sold a possession but "kept back part of the price" while claiming to give it all. Their sin was not in keeping money (which was lawful) but in lying about it to appear more generous than they were. They sought the reputation of self-sacrifice without the reality. "Satan filled his heart" to lie to the Holy Spirit - not just to men, but to God Himself.

2. **Lying to the Holy Ghost**: Peter's charge proves the deity of the Holy Spirit: "Thou hast not lied unto men, but unto God." This was not mere deception but "tempting the Spirit of the Lord" - testing whether God could see through their fraud. Henry notes that hypocrisy tries to "put a cheat upon God" but "God is not mocked."

3. **Immediate Judgment**: Both died instantly at Peter's word. This severity served to maintain the Holy Spirit's honor in the infant church and to deter others. Henry compares this to judgments at the beginning of other dispensations (golden calf, Nadab and Abihu, gathering sticks on Sabbath). "Great fear came upon all the church."

4. **The Church Flourishing**: Despite this judgment, the church prospered. Miracles multiplied - the sick were healed even by Peter's shadow passing over them. "The more they were afflicted, the more they multiplied."

5. **Second Imprisonment and Deliverance**: The high priest arrested the apostles, but an angel freed them at night with orders to "speak in the temple all the words of this life." They obeyed immediately, teaching at daybreak.

6. **Gamaliel's Counsel**: This Pharisee wisely advised caution: if this work is of men, it will fail; if of God, you cannot overthrow it. "Lest haply ye be found even to fight against God." The council followed his advice, though they still beat the apostles.

7. **Rejoicing in Suffering**: The apostles "departed from the council, rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer shame for his name." Suffering for Christ is honorable, not shameful.

Practical applications:
- Hypocrisy cannot hide from God
- God's judgments maintain reverence in the church
- Persecution cannot stop the gospel
- We should count it joy to suffer for Christ's name
