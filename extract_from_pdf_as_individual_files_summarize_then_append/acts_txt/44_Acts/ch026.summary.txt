Chapter 26: Paul Before Agrippa; The Conversion Retold; "Almost Persuaded"
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's masterful defense before King Agrippa - a full account of his life, conversion, and commission that nearly persuades the king and proves Paul innocent of all charges.

Key theological themes:
1. **Expert in Jewish Matters**: Paul was glad to appear before Agrippa, "especially because I know thee to be expert in all customs and questions which are among the Jews" - a judge who could understand the real issues.

2. **The Straitest Sect**: "After the most straitest sect of our religion I lived a Pharisee." Paul was no ignorant man, no immoral man, no heretic - but learned, virtuous, and orthodox.

3. **Hope of the Promise**: "I stand and am judged for the hope of the promise made of God unto our fathers" - the same hope Israel's twelve tribes "instantly serving God day and night" hoped to attain.

4. **Why Incredible?**: "Why should it be thought a thing incredible with you, that God should raise the dead?" The resurrection is not contrary to reason but above it - God's almighty power makes it certain.

5. **Compelled to Blaspheme**: In his persecution, Paul "punished them oft in every synagogue, and compelled them to blaspheme" - the worst injury of persecution, forcing men to sin against their consciences.

6. **The Light and Voice**: At midday, "a light from heaven, above the brightness of the sun," with Christ's voice: "Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me? it is hard for thee to kick against the pricks."

7. **The Commission**: "To open their eyes, and to turn them from darkness to light, and from the power of Satan unto God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins, and inheritance among them which are sanctified by faith that is in me."

8. **Not Disobedient**: "Whereupon, O king Agrippa, I was not disobedient unto the heavenly vision." Divine revelation demands immediate obedience; Paul preached repentance to all, beginning at Damascus.

9. **Much Learning Makes Thee Mad**: Festus interrupted: "Paul, thou art beside thyself." The wisdom of God appears as foolishness to those who perish, but Paul answered with "words of truth and soberness."

10. **Almost Persuaded**: Agrippa said, "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian." Paul replied, "I would to God, that not only thou, but also all that hear me this day, were both almost, and altogether such as I am, except these bonds."

Practical applications:
- The resurrection is the hinge of Christianity - believe it or deny all
- Those who persecute Christ's people persecute Christ himself
- Heavenly visions demand earthly obedience
- "Almost" persuaded is altogether lost
- Ministers should wish all their hearers converted, not just convinced
