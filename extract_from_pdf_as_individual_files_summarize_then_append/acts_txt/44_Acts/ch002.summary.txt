Chapter 2: The Day of Pentecost
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Pentecost as the fulfillment of Christ's promise - the Spirit descends with power upon the waiting church, enabling bold proclamation and producing remarkable conversions.

Key theological themes:
1. **Timing and Preparation**: The Spirit came on Pentecost (commemorating Sinai's law-giving), now publishing the evangelical law to all nations. The disciples were "all with one accord in one place" - unity and expectant prayer prepared them for blessing. The Spirit, like a dove, comes not where there is noise and clamour.

2. **The Signs**: A rushing mighty wind filled the house, signifying the Spirit's powerful operations that would be "mighty through God." Cloven tongues of fire appeared - fire for purification, zeal, and illumination; tongues because the Spirit would enable proclamation to all nations. The reversal of Babel: God now divides tongues to restore the knowledge of Himself.

3. **Speaking in Tongues**: The disciples spoke in languages they never learned - a miracle upon the mind. This was necessary for their commission to "preach the gospel to every creature" and proved that the sacred records should be preserved in all languages.

4. **Peter's Sermon**: Filled with the Spirit, Peter quoted Joel 2:28-32, showing this was prophesied - the Spirit poured out "upon all flesh" without distinction of sex, age, or condition. The "last days" refer to the final dispensation, and the "great and notable day of the Lord" points to Jerusalem's destruction (AD 70) - judgment on those who rejected Christ.

5. **Christ Proclaimed**: Peter boldly charged his hearers with crucifying Jesus, whom God raised and exalted. David's prophecy (Psalm 16) proves Christ's resurrection - David's tomb remained, but Christ's did not. Exalted to God's right hand, Christ poured out this Spirit.

6. **The Response**: Three thousand souls were "pricked in heart," repented, were baptized, and added to the church. They continued in apostles' doctrine, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayers.

7. **The Primitive Church**: The believers had all things common (not abolishing property but being willing to share), met daily in the temple and homes, and the Lord added daily to the church.

Practical applications:
- Unity and prayer prepare hearts for the Spirit
- The Spirit enables bold witness
- True conviction produces repentance and baptism
- Christian community involves doctrine, fellowship, and generosity
