Chapter 9: The Conversion of Saul; Peter's Miracles
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Saul's conversion as "one of the wonders of the church" - showing that neither the greatest sinner is beyond grace, nor the greatest persecutor beyond transformation into the greatest apostle.

Key theological themes:
1. **Saul the Persecutor**: "Yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples" - persecution was natural to him, his very breath. He sought letters to Damascus to arrest "any of this way." His zeal was sincere but sincerely wrong.

2. **The Light from Heaven**: Near Damascus, "suddenly there shined round about him a light from heaven" - brighter than the sun. "The devil comes to the soul in darkness; Christ comes to the soul in light." Saul fell to the earth, humbled before he could be exalted.

3. **Why Persecutest Thou Me?**: Christ's question reveals that "those who persecute the saints persecute Christ himself." What is done against believers is done against their Lord. The doubled name "Saul, Saul" shows both his deep sleep and Christ's tender concern.

4. **Kicking Against the Pricks**: Those who resist divine convictions, rebel against God's truths, and oppose his ministers "kick against the pricks" - hurting only themselves. Saul's surrender - "Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?" - marks "the first word that grace spoke in Paul."

5. **Three Days of Darkness**: Blind and fasting, Saul experienced conviction's terrors before comfort came. The scales falling from his eyes signified recovery "from the darkness of his unconverted state" and "from the darkness of his present terrors."

6. **Ananias Sent**: A "certain disciple" was used rather than an apostle, showing that "Christ would employ variety of hands in eminent services." The sign of Saul's conversion: "Behold, he prayeth." Prayer is the first mark of spiritual life.

7. **A Chosen Vessel**: Despite Ananias's fears, Christ declares Saul "a chosen vessel unto me, to bear my name before the Gentiles, and kings, and the children of Israel" - designed for both eminent services and eminent sufferings.

8. **Immediate Preaching**: Saul "straightway preached Christ in the synagogues" - in the very places he had persecuted believers. "Is not this he that destroyed them which called on this name?"

9. **Peter's Miracles**: Aeneas, paralyzed eight years, healed with the words "Jesus Christ maketh thee whole." Tabitha (Dorcas), "full of good works and almsdeeds," raised from death - "the widows showed the coats and garments which she made."

Practical applications:
- No sinner is beyond the reach of converting grace
- Genuine conversion shows itself immediately in prayer and witness
- Persecution often prepares the way for the greatest service
- Charitable deeds speak louder than words, in life and after death
