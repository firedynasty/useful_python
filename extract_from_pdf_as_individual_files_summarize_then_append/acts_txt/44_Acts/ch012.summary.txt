Chapter 12: James Martyred; Peter Delivered; Herod's Death
----------------------------------------
Henry presents the contrasting fates of persecutor and persecuted: James beheaded, Peter miraculously delivered, and Herod struck down by divine judgment.

Key theological themes:
1. **Herod's Persecution**: He "stretched forth his hands to vex certain of the church" and killed James "with the sword." He proceeded to imprison Peter "because he saw it pleased the Jews." Blood to the bloodthirsty makes them more bloodthirsty.

2. **The Martyrdom of James**: One of the inner three disciples, a "Son of Thunder," became the first apostle to die. God permitted it, perhaps to scatter the apostles from Jerusalem or to show that his work continues without any single instrument.

3. **The Church at Prayer**: "Prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him." They prayed fervently, persistently, corporately. The death of James alarmed them to greater prayer for Peter.

4. **Peter's Peace in Prison**: The night before his execution, Peter "was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains." He had committed his cause to the righteous Judge and "his soul dwells at ease."

5. **Angelic Deliverance**: An angel came, light shone, chains fell off. Peter followed through wards and iron gates that "opened of their own accord." This represents redemption - the Spirit bringing light, awakening the conscience, loosing bonds, and leading to freedom.

6. **The Prayer Meeting's Response**: At Mary's house, many were "gathered together praying." When Peter knocked, Rhoda knew his voice but "opened not the gate for gladness." They said, "Thou art mad" - then "It is his angel." Joy made them slow to believe.

7. **Herod's Pride and Fall**: At Caesarea, arrayed in royal apparel, the people shouted, "It is the voice of a god." He received the flattery and "gave not God the glory." Immediately an angel smote him; "he was eaten of worms, and gave up the ghost."

8. **The Word Prevails**: "But the word of God grew and multiplied." The persecutor perished; the gospel prospered. The church, the more afflicted, the more multiplied.

Practical applications:
- Persistent prayer prevails with God
- Peace in danger comes from trusting God's sovereignty
- Pride invites divine judgment
- Persecution cannot stop the gospel's advance
