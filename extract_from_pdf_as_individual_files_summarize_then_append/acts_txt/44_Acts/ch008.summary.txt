Chapter 8: Persecution Spreads the Gospel; Philip in Samaria
----------------------------------------
Henry shows how persecution became the means of gospel expansion, fulfilling Christ's commission to witness "in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth."

Key theological themes:
1. **Persecution Scatters the Church**: Saul "made havoc of the church," entering houses and dragging men and women to prison. Yet "the more they were afflicted, the more they multiplied." The scattered disciples "went every where preaching the word." What Satan meant to destroy, God used to spread.

2. **Philip in Samaria**: The deacon-turned-evangelist "preached Christ" to the Samaritans - those traditionally despised by Jews. "Christ sent his gospel to slay all enmities." The people "with one accord gave heed" and "there was great joy in that city." The gospel brings joy wherever it is received.

3. **Simon Magus**: This sorcerer had "bewitched" the Samaritans with his claims to be "some great one." Yet even he believed and was baptized when he saw Philip's miracles. Henry warns that some believe only superficially, impressed by signs but without heart change.

4. **The Gift of the Spirit**: Peter and John came from Jerusalem; through their laying on of hands, believers received the Holy Spirit visibly. Simon offered money for this power - hence "simony" for buying church offices. Peter's rebuke was severe: "Thy money perish with thee... thy heart is not right in the sight of God."

5. **Simon's Root Problem**: His heart was "in the gall of bitterness, and in the bond of iniquity." He wanted power for self-exaltation, not service. Yet Peter offered hope: "Repent therefore of this thy wickedness, and pray God, if perhaps the thought of thine heart may be forgiven thee."

6. **The Ethiopian Eunuch**: Philip was directed to a desert road where he found an Ethiopian official reading Isaiah 53 in his chariot. Asked "Understandest thou what thou readest?", he replied, "How can I, except some man should guide me?" Philip "preached unto him Jesus."

7. **Immediate Baptism**: Upon profession of faith - "I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God" - the eunuch was baptized immediately in available water. The Spirit then caught Philip away, and the eunuch "went on his way rejoicing."

Practical applications:
- Persecution often advances what it means to suppress
- The gospel breaks down ethnic and social barriers
- Desire for spiritual gifts from wrong motives is dangerous
- Providence arranges gospel opportunities; be ready to explain Christ
