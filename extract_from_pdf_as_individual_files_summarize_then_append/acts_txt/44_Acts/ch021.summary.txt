Chapter 21: Paul's Journey to Jerusalem; Agabus's Prophecy; Paul Seized in the Temple
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's determined journey toward suffering, the affectionate concern of friends, and the beginning of his long imprisonment - showing how God's servants must follow providence even into bonds.

Key theological themes:
1. **Friends Dissuading Paul**: At Tyre, disciples "said to Paul through the Spirit, that he should not go up to Jerusalem." At Caesarea, Agabus bound his own hands and feet with Paul's girdle, prophesying his arrest. "What mean ye to weep and to break mine heart?"

2. **Ready Not Only to Be Bound**: Paul's heroic response: "I am ready not only to be bound, but also to die at Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus." He counted his life "not dear unto himself" for Christ's service.

3. **The Will of the Lord Be Done**: When Paul would not be persuaded, his friends "ceased, saying, The will of the Lord be done." They submitted to divine providence, though grieved. "When we see trouble coming, it becomes us to say, The will of the Lord be done."

4. **Philip's Daughters**: At Caesarea they lodged with "Philip the evangelist, which was one of the seven" who "had four daughters, virgins, which did prophesy" - fulfillment of Joel's promise that sons and daughters should prophesy.

5. **Mnason, an Old Disciple**: They lodged with one "of Cyprus, an old disciple" - perhaps from Christ's time or Pentecost. "It is an honourable thing to be an old disciple of Jesus Christ, to have been enabled by the grace of God to continue long in a course of duty."

6. **Paul's Conciliation at Jerusalem**: James and the elders, noting "how many thousands of Jews there are which believe" who were "zealous of the law," advised Paul to purify himself with four men under a vow - to show he did not teach Jews "to forsake Moses."

7. **The Temple Riot**: Jews from Asia, seeing Paul in the temple, stirred up the crowd: "This is the man, that teacheth all men every where against the people, and the law, and this place." They dragged him out and would have killed him.

8. **Rescued by the Chief Captain**: Soldiers intervened; Paul was bound with chains. "The castle was indeed a protection to him, but withal it was a confinement." From this point Paul remains a prisoner to the end of Acts.

Practical applications:
- God's servants must be ready for suffering as well as service
- Divine warnings do not always mean we should avoid, but prepare
- "The will of the Lord be done" is the ultimate submission
- Old disciples deserve honor for long faithfulness
- Conciliation to weaker brethren has its limits
