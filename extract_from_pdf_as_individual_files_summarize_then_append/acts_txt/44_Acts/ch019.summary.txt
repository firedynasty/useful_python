Chapter 19: Paul at Ephesus; Disciples Rebaptized; Exorcists Shamed; Diana's Riot
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Paul's remarkable ministry at Ephesus - correcting deficient believers, performing extraordinary miracles, and triumphing over both demonic counterfeiters and idolatrous craftsmen.

Key theological themes:
1. **Disciples with John's Baptism**: Paul found about twelve who had "not so much as heard whether there be any Holy Ghost." They had been baptized "unto John's baptism" but not in Christ's name. John's baptism was good but incomplete - "the porch which you were to pass through, not the house you were to rest in."

2. **Have Ye Received the Holy Ghost?**: A searching question for all believers. "Those are baptized to no purpose that do not receive and submit to the Holy Ghost." When baptized properly in Jesus' name, Paul laid hands on them and "the Holy Ghost came on them."

3. **Three Months in the Synagogue**: Paul "spoke boldly" - arguing, persuading, preaching "the things concerning the kingdom of God." When "divers were hardened" and "spoke evil of that way before the multitude," he departed and separated the disciples.

4. **School of Tyrannus**: Paul disputed daily for two years, so that "all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus." Daily preaching reached more than sabbath-only ministry. The gate of opportunity was "wide and effectual."

5. **Special Miracles**: "God wrought special miracles by the hands of Paul" - even handkerchiefs and aprons from his body healed the sick and cast out devils. These were "not according to the common course of nature" but extraordinary confirmations.

6. **The Exorcists Shamed**: Seven sons of Sceva attempted to cast out demons "by Jesus whom Paul preacheth" - using the name without faith. The evil spirit answered: "Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?" The possessed man leaped on them, and they "fled out of that house naked and wounded."

7. **Fear and Confession**: This became known to all, "fear fell on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified." Many confessed their sins and deeds. Those who "used curious arts" burned their magic books publicly - worth fifty thousand pieces of silver. "So mightily grew the word of God and prevailed."

8. **Demetrius and Diana**: The silversmiths who made shrines for Diana saw their trade threatened. Demetrius inflamed them: "This Paul hath persuaded and turned away much people." The city was filled with confusion, crying "Great is Diana of the Ephesians" - but the town clerk dispersed them with prudent counsel.

Practical applications:
- Incomplete Christianity needs completing, not despising
- Those who use Christ's name without faith face judgment
- Genuine conversion produces public renunciation of sin
- The gospel threatens every trade built on human vice
- Worldly interest often masquerades as religious zeal
