Chapter 14: Iconium, Lystra, and Derbe; Paul Stoned
----------------------------------------
Henry traces the gospel's advance through opposition, miracles, and suffering - showing both the cost and the triumph of apostolic ministry.

Key theological themes:
1. **Success at Iconium**: "They so spoke that a great multitude both of the Jews and also of the Greeks believed." The manner of preaching matters - "so plainly, so convincingly, with such evidence and demonstration of the Spirit."

2. **Persecution and Division**: "The multitude of the city was divided" - some held with the unbelieving Jews, others with the apostles. "It is better that part of the city go to heaven than all to hell."

3. **Bold Continuance**: Despite opposition, "therefore they abode there a long time, speaking boldly in the Lord." The more they perceived the town's spite, the more they were animated to continue. "They spoke boldly in the Lord, in his strength, and trusting in him to bear them out."

4. **The Lame Man at Lystra**: Paul perceived he "had faith to be healed" and commanded, "Stand upright on thy feet. And he leaped and walked." Those "cured of their spiritual lameness must show it by leaping with a holy exultation and walking in a holy conversation."

5. **Mistaken for Gods**: The people cried, "The gods are come down to us in the likeness of men," calling Barnabas Jupiter and Paul Mercury. The priest brought oxen and garlands to sacrifice to them.

6. **The Apostles' Horror**: "They rent their clothes, and ran in among the people, crying out, Sirs, why do ye these things? We also are men of like passions with you." They preached: "Turn from these vanities unto the living God, which made heaven, and earth."

7. **Paul Stoned**: Jews from Antioch and Iconium persuaded the people; "they stoned Paul, drew him out of the city, supposing he had been dead." But he rose and departed to Derbe - "where God gives great grace he commonly exercises it with great trials."

8. **Confirming the Churches**: Returning through hostile cities, they confirmed "the souls of the disciples, and exhorted them to continue in the faith" - declaring "we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God."

Practical applications:
- Gospel preaching demands boldness, not timidity
- Miracles must point to Christ, not to human instruments
- Suffering for Christ is the pathway to glory
- New converts need confirming and establishing
