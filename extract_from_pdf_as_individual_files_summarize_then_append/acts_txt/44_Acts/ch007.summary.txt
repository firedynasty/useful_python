Chapter 7: Stephen's Defense and Martyrdom
----------------------------------------
Henry presents Stephen's masterful survey of Israel's history as demonstrating that God was never confined to one place or ceremony, and that Israel had always resisted His messengers - culminating in their rejection of Christ.

Key theological themes:
1. **Abraham's Call**: God appeared "in glory" to Abraham in Mesopotamia, not in the holy land. Stephen shows God worked outside Canaan's boundaries. Abraham had no inheritance there, only promises - pointing to "the heavenly country" as the true inheritance.

2. **Joseph Rejected Then Exalted**: The patriarchs "moved with envy, sold Joseph into Egypt" - prefiguring how Israel's leaders treated Christ. Yet "God was with him" and made him ruler. God's purposes triumph through human opposition.

3. **Moses Rejected by Israel**: Moses "supposed his brethren would have understood" that God was using him, "but they understood not." They asked, "Who made thee a ruler and a judge over us?" - the same question later asked of Christ. Israel consistently rejected their deliverers.

4. **God Not Confined to the Temple**: Moses received the law at Sinai, not in Canaan. The tabernacle was moveable. Solomon built the temple but acknowledged "the most High dwelleth not in temples made with hands." Heaven is God's throne, earth his footstool - "What house will ye build me?"

5. **The Pattern of Resistance**: "Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost: as your fathers did, so do ye." They persecuted the prophets who foretold Christ, then betrayed and murdered the "Just One" himself.

6. **Stephen's Vision**: As they gnashed on him with their teeth, "he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God." Christ stands to welcome his martyr.

7. **His Death**: They "cast him out of the city, and stoned him." His final prayers echoed Christ's: "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit" and "Lord, lay not this sin to their charge." Then "he fell asleep" - death as peaceful rest for believers.

8. **Saul's Presence**: The witnesses "laid down their clothes at a young man's feet, whose name was Saul" - introducing the great persecutor who would become the great apostle.

Practical applications:
- God's presence is not limited to sacred places
- Rejection by men cannot thwart God's purposes
- Christ stands to receive his suffering servants
- Dying grace enables faithful witness to the end
