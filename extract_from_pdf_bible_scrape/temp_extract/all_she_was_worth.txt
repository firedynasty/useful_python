About the book
In this gripping thriller by Miyuki Miyabe, readers are drawn
into a haunting exploration of Japan's consumer-driven
society, where individuals become ensnared in crippling debt
and ruthless underground lending systems. When a beautiful
young woman goes missing, a determined detective uncovers a
twisted web of deception, revealing that the woman may not
be who she appears. As layers of identity and truth unravel, the
story raises unsettling questions: Is she a victim of
circumstance, a perpetrator of crime, or perhaps both? In a
nation where surveillance is omnipresent, how can two women
share a singular identity and vanish without a trace?

Summary of Chapter 3 of "All She Was Worth"
In this chapter, we delve into the financial ruin of Shoko
Sekine, who has been struggling with debt since receiving
her first credit card in 1983. Over time, she accumulates
massive debts, leading her to file for personal bankruptcy at
the Tokyo Regional Court. Despite her attempts to manage
her finances by taking on multiple jobs, she finds herself
trapped in a vicious cycle of borrowing and repayment,
ultimately leading to a total debt of ten million yen owed to
thirty creditors. Faced with dire circumstances and no assets,
Shoko's only option seems to seek bankruptcy relief. 
Amidst Shoko's financial turmoil, her fiancé, Jun Kurisaka, is
desperate to understand her situation. After discovering her
bankruptcy letter, he confronts her, but she disappears
without a trace. Jun reaches out to Honma, a relative and
former detective, asking for help in locating Shoko. Honma
feels conflicted; while he is intrigued by the mystery, he is
also reminded of his own life’s shortcomings, particularly
regarding family obligations.
