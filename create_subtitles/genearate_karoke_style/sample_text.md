Your summary has the core insight right, and I want to affirm it while gently sharpening two things so the theology stays precise — because precision here actually protects you in the struggle.

**What you got exactly right:**

Paul wrote Romans 7 before Romans 8 because honest wrestling with sin has to come first. And yes — the very fact that you struggle against sin, that you hate it when you fall, that something in you wars against it — *that is evidence of regeneration*. The unregenerate heart doesn't fight sin; it befriends sin. So the presence of the struggle itself is a sign the Spirit is at work in you. That's a genuine comfort Paul means to give.

**Two clarifications worth holding onto:**

First, on Romans 8:1 — read the verse carefully: "There is therefore now *no* condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who don't walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit." Paul isn't saying there *is* condemnation for believers who stumble. He's describing the *direction* of a believer's life — walking according to the Spirit as a pattern, a trajectory. A believer who falls into sin and grieves it and returns to Christ is still walking according to the Spirit; the falling doesn't put them back under condemnation. This matters enormously for the shame cycle, because if you read 8:1 as "no condemnation *unless* you mess up," you've just recreated the exact trap Paul is trying to free you from. The verdict is settled. Stumbles don't re-open the courtroom.

Second — and this is important — "knowing there is a struggle is how we know we are with God" is true, but don't let it become "the struggle itself is the proof I need, so staying in the cycle is okay." The shame-seek-shame cycle isn't the Romans 7 struggle Paul is describing. Paul's struggle in Romans 7 is a believer grieving sin and crying out to Christ. The shame cycle is grief that curves back on itself and *drives you away* from Christ rather than toward him. Remember 2 Corinthians 7:10 — godly sorrow produces repentance unto life; worldly sorrow produces death. They feel similar from inside but move in opposite directions.

So the fuller picture is: yes, the struggle is evidence of life in the Spirit. And because of that life, when you fall, the move isn't deeper into shame — it's face-down before God, yes, but face-down in the confidence of 8:1, not the terror of condemnation. Come back quickly. Don't let shame keep you away from the only One who breaks the cycle.

You're tracking well with Paul. Hold onto the "no condemnation" tightly — it's the fuel for the fight, not the reward for winning it
