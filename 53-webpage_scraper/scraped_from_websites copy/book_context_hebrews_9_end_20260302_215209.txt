# Hebrews 9:1-14 Bible Study

## Passage Structure
**I. Description of the earthly tabernacle (1-5)**
**II. Weaknesses of the earthly tabernacle/system (6-10)**
**III. Our eternal redemption through Christ who entered the Holy of Holies on our behalf (11-14)**

## The Earthly Tabernacle and Worship (Verses 1-5)

### Purpose and Function
One of the key purposes of the tabernacle was to be a reminder to people about the need to worship God. Many of the objects inside were related to worship. God created us to worship Him from the beginning. In Revelation 7:9-12 we can see that God's "end vision" for the world is to make worshipers of Himself of every tribe and tongue.

### Daily Worship Application
Many times, we stress the eternal importance of sharing the gospel or building the church. Yet sometimes we forget that the even more basic daily duty we have of worshiping and praising God. This is not a "once-a-week" activity we do during church. The Psalms are full of instances of David and the other writers worshiping God privately. Worship can be public. But our worship in public will likely not be real or heartfelt unless we also worship God in our homes during our own personal time with the Lord.

### Tabernacle Components and Significance
The earthly tabernacle was a shadow of the heavenly one. Many of the objects inside have deep spiritual significance:
- **Bread of Life**: "Then Jesus declared, 'I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never go hungry, and whoever believes in me will never be thirsty.'" (John 6:35)
- **Light of the World**: "When Jesus spoke again to the people, he said, 'I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.'" (John 8:12)
- **Incense and Prayer**: "The smoke of the incense, together with the prayers of God's people, went up before God from the angel's hand." (Revelation 8:4)

## Limitations of the Old System (Verses 6-10)

### Priestly Service and Restrictions
The priests serve continually in the holy place. Part of their tasks include replacing the bread daily, lighting the lamps, and keeping the incense going. It is a continuous service before the Lord. Yet they could only enter into the Holy of Holies once per year. Then only the high priest could enter. This was the day of atonement. The priest would take blood to sprinkle onto the ark of the covenant. This was offered to cover all of the sins which had been committed unknowingly by the Israelite congregation in the past year.

### Limited Access to God
The Levitical system did not provide any direct access into God's presence for His people. Rather, it kept them away. The Holy Spirit taught concerning the tabernacle that it teaches how inaccessible God is apart from the death of Jesus Christ. By the Spirit-inspired instruction given for the Holiest of All, He was indicating that there was no way to God in the ceremonial system. Only Christ could open the way (John 14:6). The priests were limited in their access to God. Only one could go once per year. Now through Christ we can all come to God directly at any time.

### Inadequate Conscience Cleansing
The gifts and sacrifices offered in the tabernacle could not truly make people perfect in conscience. Their sins were covered, but not really taken away or replaced. These sacrifices could not take away a guilty conscience. The sacrifices merely foreshadowed what would one day be the true sacrifice: Christ.

Why couldn't the sacrifices take away a guilty conscience? They couldn't because they were external, not internal. People's hearts needed to be changed. No amount of sacrifices, washings, food, or rules for cleanliness could change a person's heart. This is clearly seen in the case of the Pharisees, who did all of these rituals, but whose hearts were like a tomb (Matthew 23:13-27).

## Christ's Perfect Sacrifice (Verses 11-14)

### The Turning Point
"But" - Here is that all-important word. Just when things looked the bleakest, Christ appeared. He didn't appear with a lot of fanfare. He just came and what He did changed the world.

### Christ as High Priest
He is the high priest of good things to come. In James 1:17 we learn that every good gift is from above. Jesus is the priest who mediates between us and God and bestows upon us the good gifts of God.

### Superior Tabernacle
Jesus entered a "greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say not of this creation." The focus is on the comparison between what Jesus did (offer atonement for us with His own blood) versus what the Levites did. Instead of offering the blood of animals, He offered His own.

### Key Characteristics of Christ's Sacrifice

**Through His Own Blood**: He sacrificed Himself in the most excruciatingly painful way imaginable.

**Once for All**: Jesus doesn't need to go into the holy place on a yearly basis. Once was enough. Jesus' blood sacrifice was so intensely effective that His blood is enough for all time. "For the life of a creature is in the blood, and I have given it to you to make atonement for yourselves on the altar; it is the blood that makes atonement for one's life." (Leviticus 17:11)

**Voluntary Offering**: Jesus offered Himself - It wasn't His responsibility. He didn't have to. He chose to because of His love for us.

**Without Blemish**: Jesus was the perfect Lamb of God, innocent and unstained by the world.

### Conscience Cleansing
Christ's sacrifice cleanses our conscience. Key biblical insights on conscience:
- "The human spirit is the lamp of the Lord that sheds light on one's inmost being." (Proverbs 20:27)
- "They show that the requirements of the law are written on their hearts, their consciences also bearing witness" (Romans 2:15)
- "Let us draw near to God with a sincere heart and with the full assurance that faith brings, having our hearts sprinkled to cleanse us from a guilty conscience" (Hebrews 10:22)

Conscience can be shaped by our environment, so it's crucial to tune it correctly according to God's Word. Parents should shape their children's consciences. The purpose of having a clean conscience is to serve the living God effectively.

## Cross-References and Supporting Passages

**True Worship**: "God is spirit, and his worshipers must worship in the Spirit and in truth." (John 4:24)

**Living Sacrifice**: "Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God's mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God—this is your true and proper worship." (Romans 12:1)

**Christ's Atoning Work**: "He himself bore our sins in his body on the cross, so that we might die to sins and live for righteousness; by his wounds you have been healed." (1 Peter 2:24)

**Universal Redemption**: "And they sang a new song, saying, 'You are worthy to take the scroll and to open its seals, because you were slain, and with your blood you purchased for God persons from every tribe and language and people and nation.'" (Revelation 5:9)

## Application Questions

- In what ways did the tabernacle aid worship? How do we worship God today?
- What did the priests have to do before entering the outer tabernacle?
- When could they go into the holy place? Who could go in?
- What sins was the high priest making atonement for?
- Why can the sacrifices offered not fully restore the guilty party's conscience?
- What two thoughts does the "but" serve to highlight the contrast between?
- What are the good things that are to come through Christ?
- Should parents shape their children's consciences? How?
- Do you ever ignore your conscience? What happens if you keep ignoring it?
- What do you want to do to serve the living God in the coming week?

This passage demonstrates the superiority of Christ's sacrifice over the Old Testament system, showing how Jesus provides direct access to God, complete forgiveness, and a cleansed conscience for all believers.

# Hebrews 9:15-28 Bible Study: Christ's Superior Sacrifice

## Main Outline
I. Christ's death confirms the new covenant (15-17)
II. Moses used blood to ratify the Mosaic covenant (18-22)
III. Christ's sacrifice is superior (23-28)

## Key Discussion Questions
- For what reason is Christ the mediator of a new covenant?
- What sins does this passage say Christ's death redeemed?
- What is the first covenant that is referred to here?
- Who are those who have been called?
- What does verse 15 show us about salvation in the Old Testament?
- What does verse 16 mean? Why does there need to be death when a covenant is made?
- How can we understand the statement: "A covenant is valid only when men are dead, for it is never in force while the one who made it lives?"

## Cross-References
1 Timothy 2:5 – For there is one God and one mediator between God and mankind, the man Christ Jesus.

Romans 8:34 – Who then is the one who condemns? No one. Christ Jesus who died—more than that, who was raised to life—is at the right hand of God and is also interceding for us.

## Section I: Christ as Mediator of the New Covenant (verses 15-17)

### Christ's Unique Role as Mediator
Christ is the mediator of a New Covenant because it was His blood which was shed for us. He obtained this privilege and responsibility because He was the only one who could mediate this covenant. He is the God-man. Because He is God, He has no sin and He has the ability to take away our sins. Because He is man, he can be our representative. It is Christ that bridged the gap between man and God.

**Application**: The Christian life is not about going to church and reading the Bible and praying. These are means to an end, not the object itself. The end is to develop our relationship with Jesus. As we learn in John 15, apart from Him we can do nothing.

### Redemption Across Time
Jesus' death redeems the people who sinned even during the Mosaic covenant. This addresses the natural question: "How could people in the Old Testament be saved?" The answer is that it was never the lamb sacrifices or blood that saved people in the Old Testament. These were a foreshadow of Jesus' sacrifice. This verse makes it very clear that Jesus' sacrifice was applied even to sins which had already been committed and even to people who had already died.

In the New Testament era we are saved by looking back at Christ's sacrifice. In the Old Testament people could also be saved, but by looking forward to Jesus' sacrifice in faith. God can act outside of time and therefore Jesus' sacrifice can be applied to past, present, and future sins.

### Understanding Covenant and Testament
Verses 16-17 appear confusing at first glance. Why should death of the covenant maker be necessary for the covenant to be enforced? The word translated as "covenant" is more general and can mean "testament." In this context it means something like "last will and testament." For a will, it only goes into effect when the person who made it dies. These verses show that Jesus' death was necessary or else God's promises (even those made in the Old Testament) couldn't take effect.

## Section II: Blood and the Mosaic Covenant (verses 18-22)

### Key Questions
- What book was sprinkled?
- What was the importance of sprinkling with blood? What was the symbolism of it?
- How would you respond to an unbeliever who asks: "Why do Christians obsess over blood? How can the blood of a man who lived 2000 years ago help you?"

### Cross-References
Exodus 24:1-8 – Shows the ratification of the Mosaic covenant required shedding of blood.
Matthew 26:28 – This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins.
Leviticus 17:11 – For the life of a creature is in the blood, and I have given it to you to make atonement for yourselves on the altar; it is the blood that makes atonement for one's life.
Ephesians 1:7 – In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of sins, in accordance with the riches of God's grace.

### The Significance of Blood
Blood was used to ratify the Mosaic covenant. When God made a covenant with Abraham, animals were also killed to ratify the covenant. Blood represents life. Sin is horrible and a horrible price must be paid for sin. Using blood shows that no half-measures will do. Sin cannot just be swept under the rug or ignored. The blood reminds us that someone must pay and they must pay with their life.

The disgusting nature of sacrifices is a vivid reminder of the cost of sin. Anyone who had to offer an animal for their own sins or saw it offered would be seriously deterred from sinning again. The visceral experience would never be forgotten by those who witnessed it.

**Verse 22**: This well-known verse shows that blood is necessary for forgiveness. Blood must be shed. Someone must pay. It says "almost be said" probably because there were some other modes of cleansing, including water and fire.

## Section III: Christ's Superior Sacrifice (verses 23-28)

### Key Questions
- What does the "copies of the things in the heavens" refer to?
- What did Jesus offer when He approached the presence of God on our behalf?
- What practical impact does the fact that Jesus need only die once have on us?
- When was the consummation of the ages?
- Will you face judgment? What will happen at your judgment?
- How does the fact that there is a judgment after death affect our life on earth now?
- Why is it appointed for man to die?
- Explain the phrase "appear a second time for salvation without reference to sin."
- Are you waiting for Him? How should we wait for Him?

### Cross-References on Judgment
Romans 14:12 – So then, each of us will give an account of ourselves to God.
1 Corinthians 4:5 – Therefore judge nothing before the appointed time; wait until the Lord comes. He will bring to light what is hidden in darkness and will expose the motives of the heart.
Matthew 12:36-37 – But I tell you that everyone will have to give account on the day of judgment for every empty word they have spoken.

### Cross-References on Second Coming
Matthew 24:27 – For as lightning that comes from the east is visible even in the west, so will be the coming of the Son of Man.
Matthew 24:42-44 – Therefore keep watch, because you do not know on what day your Lord will come... So you also must be ready, because the Son of Man will come at an hour when you do not expect him.

### Superiority of Christ's Sacrifice
Verses 23-24 again compare Christ's sacrifice with the animal sacrifices in the Old Testament. The tabernacle in the Old Testament was cleansed with the blood of animal sacrifices. Christ appears in God's presence in heaven on our behalf, offering His own blood for us. His sacrifice is better because He is God's own Son—eternal, all-powerful, all-knowing, all-wise, and sinless.

**Application for Deterring Sin**: We need to realize that Jesus faced real pain for every single sin we ever commit. Jesus took the punishment for us—every bit of punishment we deserved. If we commit 100 sins in our life, Jesus took that amount of punishment. If we commit 1000 sins, Jesus took that amount of punishment. Next time you are considering whether or not to sin, remind yourself that if you do sin, your punishment is heaped on Christ. If sacrificing an animal would deter us from sinning, how much more God's one and only Son, the perfect Creator of the universe?

### Once-for-All Sacrifice
Verses 25-26: Jesus' sacrifice is once-for-all. He doesn't need to do it year by year. It doesn't need to be repeated. He suffered once at the consummation of all ages. Jesus' death is the climax of human history. Everything before it was building up to it. Everything since is affected by it.

**Applications**:
1. **Our salvation is secure** – A new sin that we do will not require that Christ be crucified again. Our salvation is complete. Don't try to make up for your sins by earning salvation.

2. **We must work to remember it** – For animal sacrifices they had to do them again and again, giving them a continual reminder of the cost of sin. For us it could be easy to forget about the cost of sin because of the time gap. Don't forget—forgetting the cost of sin removes one obstacle from sinning.

### Death and Judgment
**Verse 27**: It is appointed for us to die once. Death is part of God's plan for us. Apart from His second coming, there is no avoiding it. Why did God appoint everyone to die?

- Death is punishment for sin as God told Adam and Eve in the garden
- Death is also a blessing in disguise—it guarantees that we need not live forever under the curse of sin. Death can be a new beginning, meaning that those with eternal life can live forever in God's presence without the consequences of sin.

**Judgment**: Understanding that judgment is coming should affect our life on earth now. We will give an account of ourselves to God.

### The Second Coming
**Verse 28a**: His second coming is not about coming to save us from our sin. His first sacrifice already accomplished this. Jesus on the cross said, "It is finished." His second coming actually verifies that His sacrifice was accepted, similar to how the high priest coming back out of the Holy of Holies showed that his sacrifice on behalf of the people was accepted by God.

**Verse 28b**: The salvation is for those who eagerly wait for Him. This implies that all true believers should fall into this category. When you wait for somebody, you are thinking about their coming and preparing for their coming.

**Application**: Are you eagerly waiting? Are you prepared? Consider what you need to do to get to the point where you can say, "I am ready." Then do it!

Matthew 24:14 states: "And this gospel of the kingdom will be proclaimed throughout the whole world as a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come." Theoretically you can speed His return by sharing the gospel.

**Hebrews 10:1-25 Bible Study: Jesus' Perfect Sacrifice and Our Response**

**Main Structure:**
I. Jesus' sacrifice is complete and perfect (10:1-10)
II. Christ perfects the sanctified (10:11-18)
III. Application: Draw near to God in faith and a sincere heart (10:19-25)

**Section 1: Jesus' Sacrifice is Complete and Perfect (10:1-10)**

**Key Discussion Questions:**
- In what way is the law a shadow of the good things to come? For example, in the law what is the shadow of Jesus' sacrifice?
- What can the law not do?
- How does verse 2 prove the conclusion in verse 1 that the law cannot make perfect those who draw near?
- What does verse 3 say is a reason for those continual sacrifices?
- Why does God not desire sacrifices? What does He desire then?

**Supporting Scripture:**
Psalms 51:17 – "My sacrifice, O God, is a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart you, God, will not despise."

Isaiah 1:11 – "The multitude of your sacrifices – what are they to me?" says the Lord. "I have more than enough of burnt offerings, of rams and the fat of fattened animals; I have no pleasure in the blood of bulls and lambs and goats."

Isaiah 29:13 – "These people come near to me with their mouth and honor me with their lips, but their hearts are far from me. Their worship of me is based on merely human rules they have been taught."

**Verse by Verse Commentary:**

**Verses 1-4:** These verses review previous chapters, emphasizing that priests had to continually offer sacrifices daily with no sign of ending. The law could not perfect anyone or solve the problem of sin. The law was given as a shadow of "good things to come" - specifically, the sacrifices were a shadow of Christ's death which would once and for all atone for sins. Verse 3 reveals another purpose of the law: to be a continual reminder of sin. The daily sacrifices were a visible and gruesome reminder about the cost of sin and the necessity of it being punished.

The writer introduces a logical argument for why the law and sacrifices are inadequate. They were made yearly, referencing the yearly day of atonement. If the atonement was sufficient to solve the sin problem, it wouldn't need repetition, but every year it had to be made anew, demonstrating its inadequacy.

**Verses 5-6:** These verses show God does not delight in sacrifices. The sacrifices are means to an end, acting as object lessons to teach the seriousness of sin and instill appropriate fear about sin's results. Throughout Israelite history, sacrifices became the "end" or goal themselves.

People disobeyed God, rebelled against His teachings and prophets, harbored sins in their hearts, and thought offering sacrifices would make everything acceptable. However, sacrifices were external rituals supposed to show internal remorse over sin. When offered without the right heart attitude, they are unacceptable to God.

**Modern Church Parallels:**
A. **Giving as ritual** - Some give thinking it will remove sin or earn absolution. Biblical examples include Saul saving sheep to give to God against God's command, and Judas returning his 30 pieces of silver.

B. **Attending church as ritual** - Going because "it's Sunday," for social reasons, or to avoid criticism rather than genuine worship.

**Verses 7-9:** God didn't want sacrifice but obedience - following His will as Jesus demonstrated (Matthew 26:39, 42). These verses can be taken as prophecy about Jesus, with the "I" referring to the Messiah. Jesus fulfilled the Old Covenant by obeying God's will from the heart where all others had failed.

**Application:** Always evaluate your heart and motives for serving God. Ask: "Why am I going to church today?" "Why am I reading the Bible?" "Why should I pray before meals?" Pray before church asking God to prepare your heart for learning.

**Verse 10:** We have been sanctified. His once-for-all sacrifice has made us positionally holy before God. Jesus accomplished what the law never could. Although we are positionally sanctified, there remains the ongoing process of becoming more like Christ in daily practice.

**Section 2: Christ Perfects the Sanctified (10:11-18)**

**Key Discussion Questions:**
- What is the significance that Christ sat down at the right hand of God after His sacrifice?
- What is He waiting for? Why not subject all enemies now?
- Explain the importance: "perfected for all time those who are sanctified"
- How can we better allow God's law to be on our minds and heart?

**Supporting Scripture:**
Psalm 110:1 – "The Lord says to my Lord: 'Sit at My right hand until I make Your enemies a footstool for Your feet.'"

**Verses on Sanctification:**
2 Timothy 2:21 – "Those who cleanse themselves from the latter will be instruments for special purposes, made holy, useful to the Master and prepared to do any good work."

1 Thessalonians 5:23 – "May God himself, the God of peace, sanctify you through and through. May your whole spirit, soul and body be kept blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ."

**Verse by Verse Commentary:**

**Verses 11-12:** The contrast is striking - priests stand because their work isn't finished, needing endless repetition. Jesus sat down at God's right hand because His sacrificial work is complete. You sit down after finishing work. Jesus NEVER has to repeat His sacrifice.

**Verse 13:** Jesus waits for the right time to return and establish His earthly kingdom when all enemies will bow and confess He is Messiah. Although His sacrificial work is completed, He's not done with world history.

History records great empires - Rome's power, Babylon's splendor, Greek wisdom, Egyptian influence, Chinese creativity. One coming empire will surpass all others in power, splendor, wisdom, peace, and might: Jesus' kingdom. What is He waiting for?

2 Peter 3:9 – "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance."

**Verses 14-16:** He has perfected us! Application: Live like it! God wants to put His law on our hearts and minds. While He does this work, we have responsibility to respond by:
- Reading, studying, and memorizing His Words
- Routinely confessing sin to remove relationship barriers
- Sharpening our minds spiritually through meditation and deeper study

**Verses 17-18:** He remembers our sins no more. This is the gospel result in a nutshell. In this world, crimes go on permanent records. In rare cases, judges may purge records - that's what God does for us. He purges our record and will never hold those sins against us, removing them "as far as the East is from the West" (Psalm 103:12).

**Section 3: Application - Draw Near to God (10:19-25)**

**Key Discussion Questions:**
- What does it mean that we can enter the holy place?
- How can we better hold fast our hope without wavering?
- What can you do to help other believers grow?
- What is the danger in NOT meeting with other believers?
- What should we be doing when we meet?

**Supporting Scripture on Fellowship:**
1 Thessalonians 5:11 – "Therefore encourage one another and build each other up, just as in fact you are doing."

Proverbs 27:17 – "As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another."

Acts 2:44-47 – "All the believers were together and had everything in common. They sold property and possessions to give to anyone who had need. Every day they continued to meet together in the temple courts. They broke bread in their homes and ate together with glad and sincere hearts, praising God and enjoying the favor of all the people."

**Verse by Verse Commentary:**

**Verses 19-21:** Concise summary of main points from recent chapters:
- We can enter the holy place (God's presence) by Christ's blood
- He has given us a new covenant
- He tore down the veil through His body's sacrifice
- We have a great high priest

**Practical Applications Based on These Truths:**

**A. Draw near with a sincere heart** - We have a great high priest offering grace, love, mercy, and compassion. Draw near to Him and He will draw near to you. God cares more about heart attitude than external appearances.

**B. In full assurance of faith** - Do not waver or doubt. We can have confidence in Him.

**C. Hearts sprinkled clean from evil conscience and bodies washed with pure water** - If we belong to Christ, this is true about us. Don't return to former life. Don't love the world. Put on the new, toss off the old. Quickly repent of sins without allowing them foothold. Be grateful for God's forgiveness and be a true worshiper.

**D. Hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering** - Stand firm. Don't allow suffering, persecution, or worldly temptations to weaken your relationship with Christ. Satan attacks little by little through small compromises and defeats.

**Illustration:** Gandalf's words to soldiers in Lord of the Rings: "Stand firm! You are soldiers of Gondor! No matter what comes through those gates, hold your ground!" For us: stand firm as soldiers of Christ. We can stand firm not through our own power but because He is faithful, fulfilling every promise.

**E. Consider how to stimulate one another to love and good deeds** - This famous verse on Christian fellowship uses active voice. Don't wait for other Christians to befriend you or invite you. Take initiative to reach out, meet new Christians, develop relationships, offer hospitality. During these times, seek ways to encourage and help each other spiritually.

**F. Not forsaking meeting together** - Don't live your Christian life in isolation - that's a recipe for failure. God made us social beings. Following Christ is difficult even with help from other believers; without them it's nearly impossible (since trying alone means disobeying this command). Meet with believers not only in church but throughout the week. Church often lacks opportunities for in-depth discussion or encouragement. Smaller meetings allow personal connection and comfortable mutual investment in each other's lives.

The goal of studying God's Word is life change - consider how to apply these principles in specific ways in coming days and weeks.

# Hebrews 9:26-39 Bible Study

## I. Christ's sacrifice should motivate us to righteous living (26-31)
## II. Jesus is worth suffering for as they had done (32-39)

### Key Discussion Questions

**On Perseverance Through Suffering (verses 32-39):**
- Who is the "you" in these verses? What events do you think the writer is referring to?
- Who in the Bible was made a public spectacle? (Samson, Jesus)
- What was their attitude in the midst of suffering?
- How does your suffering for Christ compare to theirs?
- What is the better possession that they had? What other qualities of this were mentioned (it is lasting)?

### Cross-References

**1 John 3:4-10** – John warns us about practicing sin.

**Romans 14:12-13** – "So then, each of us will give an account of ourselves to God. Therefore let us stop passing judgment on one another. Instead, make up your mind not to put any stumbling block or obstacle in the way of a brother or sister."

**Revelation 20:11-15** – Great White Throne Judgment.

**1 Peter 1:17** – "Since you call on a Father who judges each person's work impartially, live out your time as foreigners here in reverent fear."

**Romans 5:3-5** – "Not only so, but we also glory in our sufferings, because we know that suffering produces perseverance; perseverance, character; and character, hope. And hope does not put us to shame, because God's love has been poured out into our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us."

**1 Peter 5:10** – "And the God of all grace, who called you to his eternal glory in Christ, after you have suffered a little while, will himself restore you and make you strong, firm and steadfast."

**Romans 8:18** – "I consider that our present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that will be revealed in us."

### Verse by Verse Commentary

**On Willful Sin (verses 26-31):**

Some people presume upon the grace of God. They claim license by doing whatever they like and then expecting that God will forgive them. We know that God can and does forgive. But there is a condition: genuine repentance. In verse 26, the writer warns those who sin deliberately. They plan to do it. They know they are going to do it. And they plan to offer up some prayer of confession afterward before they go away and do it again. For such a person who intentionally sins and willfully disregards Christ's sacrifice, His sacrifice is not applied to them. They have not genuinely confessed and are therefore not forgiven or covered by the blood of Christ. Instead, such a person should expect judgment. Such a person should be afraid of the furious fire of judgment to come.

The writer gives an example from the Old Testament. Someone living in Old Testament times who breaks the law is judged without mercy, often to death. How much more sinful is it to spurn Jesus' sacrifice on our behalf. Grace should motivate us to serve God with a happy heart. But those people who are motivated by grace to sin more have profaned Jesus' blood and have another thing coming.

Things may seem to go on with no problems for a while, but God will not allow these sins to go unpunished. He will take vengeance upon those who so disrespect what His Son went through on the cross.

**On Perseverance Through Suffering (verses 32-39):**

The author reminds them of their previous willingness to face suffering. As they diligently followed after the Lord, they faced all kinds of trials and difficulties and persecutions, but they joyfully accepted it knowing what God had in store for them. Thus, the saints are encouraged to remember their previous passion for the gospel and self-sacrifice in being willing to face anything for the Lord. Lest they start to lose endurance and give up, the writer seeks to encourage them by reminding them of their previous godly attitude.

God has promised great things to those who endure to the end without giving up. He will return and He will preserve the souls of the faithful. He will bring about everything good which He has promised. For His part, He will not fail.

Therefore, the saints are encouraged to have faith and persevere and "not shrink back." Think about your own lives for a moment. How excited were you when you first started following after Christ? How diligent were you in Bible study? Are you maintaining that same excitement and diligence you used to have? Or are there certain things you have quit and given up on?

**Hebrews 11:1-7 Bible Study: We Must Have Faith to Please God**

**Chapter Context and Structure**
This chapter connects directly to chapter 10 and leads into chapter 12:1, forming a crucial bridge in the book's argument about faith's essential role in the Christian life. Hebrews 11 serves as the definitive biblical exposition on faith in action through the examples of Old Testament patriarchs.

**Definition and Nature of Faith**
Faith is defined as "assurance" of things hoped for and "conviction" of things not seen. This biblical faith is neither irrational nor blind. God provides evidence of His existence through creation (Psalm 19) and other means. Jesus often provided evidence for belief, most notably "the Sign of Jonah." When Gideon asked for signs, God graciously provided multiple confirmations.

Faith is reasonable and logical, though not 100% provable. While there are substantial evidences and reasons to believe in God's existence, some element of faith remains necessary. Faith involves being convinced of something unseen, supported by evidence but requiring trust beyond complete proof.

Verse 2 demonstrates this principle: "by faith we understand that the universe was created by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that are visible."

**Old Testament Examples of Faith**

**Abel (Genesis 4:1-15)**
Abel's sacrifice was superior to Cain's because God accepted it while rejecting Cain's offering. Though the Bible doesn't explicitly detail God's instructions about sacrifices, it's reasonable to assume God had communicated His expectations. Cain's offering represented the first instance of man-made religion, similar to Aaron's sons who offered "strange fire" before the Lord (Leviticus 10:1). Abel's faith demonstrated proper worship according to God's standards.

**Enoch (Genesis 5:18-24)**
The text describes Enoch as one who "walked with God" during the pre-flood era when "the thoughts of people's hearts were evil continually." This was arguably a more wicked generation than today, far removed from the Garden of Eden. Stories from Adam were becoming distant memories, and direct divine interaction was rare.

While most people mocked and scorned the unseen God, Enoch remained faithful. He "followed the light when most people sought to extinguish it." As a result, God took him directly to heaven without experiencing death.

**Universal Nature of Faith**
Everyone exercises faith daily; the crucial question is where that faith is placed. Scientists who teach evolution make numerous unsubstantiated assumptions: that something (the Big Bang) came from nothing, that life began randomly without explanation of when, where, or how, and that energy arose from non-energy independently.

Daily life requires constant faith: trusting airline pilots never met, riding with Uber drivers on busy roads, sitting on chairs expecting them to hold weight, or assuming salary deposits appear without verification. Those who deny faith's necessity deceive themselves.

**Faith in Action vs. Lack of Faith**
Hebrews 11 presents numerous examples of believers exercising extraordinary faith in challenging circumstances. Similar examples exist today among modern believers who demonstrate remarkable trust in God through their actions.

Conversely, certain behaviors reveal lack of faith: compromising moral standards to preserve employment instead of trusting God's provision, excessive worry about the future, or relying solely on human understanding without prayer.

**Key Cross-References on Faith**
- Matthew 7:21: "Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father who is in heaven."
- Galatians 5:6: "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision has any value. The only thing that counts is faith expressing itself through love."

- Romans 1:17: "For in the gospel the righteousness of God is revealed—a righteousness that is by faith from first to last, just as it is written: 'The righteous will live by faith.'"
- Romans 10:10: "For it is with your heart that you believe and are justified, and it is with your mouth that you profess your faith and are saved."

**Extended Faith Passages**
- James 2:14-26: Classic teaching on faith and works relationship
- Luke 6:46-49: Building on solid foundation requires doing what Christ says
- Luke 7:1-8: The centurion's extraordinary faith

**Philosophical Insight**
Henry David Thoreau observed: "If I seem to walk out of step with others, it is because I am listening to another drum beat." This captures faith's essence—believers walk to a different rhythm, guided by unseen realities rather than visible circumstances.

The chapter challenges readers to examine where they place their faith and whether their actions align with professed beliefs. Growing in faith requires moving beyond intellectual assent to active trust in God's character and promises, even when circumstances suggest otherwise. The Old Testament examples serve as ongoing inspiration for contemporary believers facing their own faith challenges.

**Key Takeaway:**
Faith requires welcoming God's promises "from a distance" and sacrificing everything for God, even when complete fulfillment isn't witnessed in one's lifetime. These examples demonstrate faith as active hope and conviction in unseen realities, choosing eternal significance over temporary worldly satisfaction.

# Hebrews 12:1-11 Bible Study: Running the Race with Endurance and God's Discipline

## I. Run the Race with Endurance (12:1-3)

### The Great Cloud of Witnesses

The "therefore" in verse 1 directly connects to Hebrews 11, where the author presented examples of faithful Old Testament saints. These believers form a "so great a cloud of witnesses" - not necessarily actively watching us from heaven, but providing powerful examples that their lives demonstrated it was possible to face trials and adversities in this world and conquer them by faith. They were not "super-believers" who never sinned, but faithful believers who chose to have faith in God even when it wasn't easy.

**Illustration**: Imagine a rookie baseball player at Boston's Fenway Park, getting ready for his first game. His coach reminds him of all the baseball greats who have stood in that same spot beside home plate. This inspires the rookie to do his absolute best to follow in the footsteps of baseball heroes. Similarly, the writer motivates believers by showing it is possible to live faithful lives even in the midst of a crooked and dark generation.

### Laying Aside Every Encumbrance

An encumbrance is not necessarily a sin - it's something that slows an athlete down in a race. 

**Illustration**: A marathon runner arrives at the starting line with a large backpack containing a CD player, portable fan, extra clothes, and other "good" things. Are these bad? Not necessarily, but they are unnecessary and slow him down.

The Christian life requires evaluating what activities we may need to cut out to be most productive. We should be willing to say "no" to things that may encumber us, even when they aren't sinful. Do not allow "good" things to keep you from the best thing.

### The Sin Which Easily Entangles

Sin is sneaky and easy to begin tolerating. It builds a wall between us and God, keeping us from being effective in service. We must be quick to recognize sin and repent of it.

### Running with Endurance

The Christian life is not a sprint, but a marathon. Each saint in Hebrews 11 faced many challenges, trials, and temptations. Sometimes they stumbled, but they got back up and finished the race. Unfortunately, many professing believers fall away. Statistics show that most Christian workers (pastors or missionaries) leave the ministry at some point, reminding us how difficult it is to persevere.

### Fixing Our Eyes on Jesus

The solution to developing endurance is found in fixing our eyes on Jesus. We must not be like Peter who took his eyes off Jesus when walking on water, saw the crashing waves, and began to sink when his faith faltered. The same will happen if we focus on circumstances and the world around us instead of on Jesus.

Jesus is our perfect example of endurance. Throughout His ministry He was doubted, scoffed at, mocked, challenged, and persecuted. Finally, He faced the most shameful, humiliating, and painful death imaginable. He endured all this "for the joy set before Him" - likely referring to His bride, the church. Jesus' death redeemed us to Himself. Our salvation was worthy enough that Jesus would face every indignation and suffering to accomplish our salvation.

**Application**: We should consider and meditate on Jesus' example. This should prevent us from growing weary or losing heart. We should persevere in whatever God is calling us to do.

## II. God Disciplines His Sons (12:4-11)

### The Context of Discipline

Verse 4 reminds readers that none of them had yet given their lives (been martyred) for their faith. They had not paid the ultimate price or been tested with the ultimate test yet. If the saints in Hebrews 11 could endure to the end, some to the point of martyrdom, so should they.

### The Motivation Behind Discipline

Some readers had forgotten about God's discipline, perhaps surprised to face consequences of sin. Maybe they had been taught that belief in God guaranteed health, wealth, and happiness. The author first establishes that the motivation of discipline is love. Teaching about God's discipline is not incompatible with teaching about God's love - it shows another aspect and the depth of His love.

The most important exhortation is: "do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord." We shouldn't cast it aside or be surprised when we face it. Instead of fainting ("I can't bear it!"), we should take it as a reminder that we are His children and correct the issue that needs addressing.

### The Nature of Discipline

The trials these believers faced may have been discipline from God. Discipline has two meanings:
1. Punishment for correction against one who has sinned
2. Training (like being disciplined to wake up at 5:00am daily for exercise)

These sufferings may have been direct results of sin, or they may fall into the general category of trials God uses to train believers to endure and strengthen their faith.

### Universal Discipline

"What son is there whom his father does not discipline?" implies that all parents SHOULD discipline their children. While the main thrust isn't teaching parents to discipline children, this is a secondary application. If parents love their children, they must discipline them. It is loving TO discipline because love is willing to do what is necessary for others' good, no matter how difficult or inconvenient.

Verse 8 indicates that if we are never disciplined by God, we don't belong to Him.

### Results of Proper Discipline

Verse 9 implies that children will respect parents who faithfully discipline them. Contrary to worldly thinking, discipline doesn't hurt the parent-child relationship or create resentment. Children have security when they know boundaries that are consistently enforced. They see their parents' love through faithful discipline.

We must subject ourselves to God, accepting correction and submitting to His authority from our hearts. This shows clearly one aspect of our relationship with God - He is not merely a friend or powerful Santa Claus, but our authority to whom we must submit.

### The Purpose of Discipline

Verses 10-11 reveal discipline's purpose. God's discipline is not punitive, shameful, hurtful, vengeful, or an outlet for anger. The goal is correction and holiness.

For parents, this means discipline must be combined with clear teaching aimed at the heart, not just external behavior. For believers, this is comforting - God doesn't discipline because He enjoys it, but always for our good so we become more holy.

Verse 11 acknowledges that experiencing or giving discipline is not pleasant, but it bears fruit. It accomplishes its purpose by changing hearts and lives. Notice "trained" indicates a continuous process, not a one-time event.

**Colossians 1:11**: "Being strengthened with all power according to his glorious might so that you may have great endurance and patience."

## Cross-References on Discipline

**Proverbs 12:1**: "Whoever loves discipline loves knowledge, but whoever hates correction is stupid."

**Proverbs 6:23**: "For this command is a lamp, this teaching is a light, and correction and instruction are the way to life."

**2 Timothy 3:12**: "In fact, everyone who wants to live a godly life in Christ Jesus will be persecuted."

**Philippians 1:29**: "For it has been granted to you on behalf of Christ not only to believe in him, but also to suffer for him."

## Applications

1. **For Running the Race**: Evaluate what "good" things might be encumbrances in your spiritual race. Practice fixing your eyes on Jesus daily through prayer, Scripture reading, and meditation on His example.

2. **For Discipline**: When facing trials, consider whether God might be disciplining you for correction or training. Submit to His authority and seek to learn from the experience.

3. **For Parents**: Discipline children out of love, combining correction with clear teaching, aiming for heart change rather than mere behavioral compliance.

4. **For Endurance**: Remember that the Christian life is a marathon requiring perseverance. Draw strength from the examples of faithful believers who have gone before us.

## Detailed Commentary

**1. Divine Discipline as Motivation**
Knowing that God disciplines those who sin should motivate us to action. Discipline is good—it shows us that we are God's children and is designed to correct us. If we don't sin, then there is no need for correction and no need to experience the sorrow connected with discipline. If we have already sinned, it would be better to repent quickly so that we won't have to remain under God's hand of discipline long.

**2. Spiritual Body Language (verses 12-13)**
These verses refer to our spiritual lives in figurative language using body parts: hands, knees, feet, limbs, joints. These refer to any area of our lives that has a problem and needs correcting. The end of verse 13 says "but rather be healed." The goal of discipline is correction and restoration. We should take the steps necessary to correct our sin and repent so that we will be restored in our relationship with God. This means addressing sin in every aspect of our lives.

**3. Pursuing Peace and Sanctification (verse 14)**
This verse shows a key point of this chapter. We are to take the warnings about discipline to heart, meaning the direction of our life has to change. We must pursue peace with all men—this doesn't happen naturally. We have to make an effort and take initiative. This might mean calling up an old friend and apologizing for a past mistake, or letting go of some resentment and forgiving someone who wronged us. 

In addition to our relationship with others, the author encourages us to pursue a closer, holier relationship with God by being sanctified. While God is the one who sanctifies us, we are not third-party bystanders but participants. It is our responsibility to respond to the conviction and leading of the Spirit by rooting out sin in our hearts. Do not be content with your current level in your walk with God. Always push forward to seek to grow closer to Him and become more like Him.

**4. God's Grace and Our Response**
God's grace is completely free. We could never deserve it. He doesn't look for a worthy recipient since none of us are worthy. As recipients of God's grace, we are called to a high standard of living—to live our lives worthily of this high calling.

Some people treat God's grace like pigs treat pearls. They cast grace aside without a second thought, take advantage of it or take it for granted. This is very dangerous. Casting aside the grace of God could allow a seed of bitterness to take hold, which could then grow and spread until it affects many in the church.

**5. Esau's Warning Example**
The author gives Esau as an example of someone who didn't appreciate God's blessings. He spurned his own birthright, lived for the moment without considering the future. His god was his stomach; his fleshly desires drove him. Later he regretted this, but it was too late. This verse reminds us that many may feel regret or sorrow about past decisions (including rejecting God), but that normally will not lead to true repentance.

Like we learned in Hebrews 6, for those who have been exposed to God's grace and shared in the blessings that His people receive, but finally reject God's offer of grace, it is impossible to renew them again to repentance. The warning is to not presume upon God's grace. Do not think you will repent or follow God later, someday in the future. Sin should not be messed around with. Repent immediately. Serve God and seek God today, not tomorrow, for tomorrow may be too late.

This section contrasts the Old and New Covenants. One of the chief themes of Hebrews is the superiority of Jesus. One proof is that the covenant which He mediated is better than the Old Covenant. These verses show some of the ways that the New Covenant is superior.

**2. Mount Sinai Experience (verses 18-21)**
The author describes a real event when God descended upon Mt. Sinai. The people were not allowed to step foot on the mountain or they would die. God's presence was clouded by a storm and announced by a trumpet call. It was a terrifying experience because God's power and holiness were awesome. The people couldn't bear it and later requested that only Moses approach God so that they wouldn't have to experience this again. This Mt. Zion doesn't refer to the actual mountain in Israel. Rather it refers more generally to the kingdom of God. He deserves it.

# Hebrews Chapter 13: Final Reminders About True Love (verses 1-8)

## Overview and Structure

The final chapter of Hebrews transitions from the lofty theological concepts of earlier chapters to practical applications for daily Christian living. After establishing Jesus' superiority and building the foundation of Christian faith, the author provides concrete guidance on how believers should live, emphasizing that believing in Jesus must affect daily interactions with both strangers and those closest to us.

## Key Themes and Applications

### Continuing Love Among Believers (verse 2)
The chapter opens with an exhortation to "let love of the brethren continue." This echoes Paul's teaching in 1 Corinthians 13 about love being the greatest attribute alongside faith and hope. Love should be the defining characteristic of Christian fellowship, visible to unbelievers who observe the church community.

### Hospitality to Strangers (verse 2-3)
The text provides unexpected reasoning for showing hospitality to strangers: believers might unknowingly be entertaining angels. This teaching reveals several important insights:

**About Angels:**
- Angels can appear as ordinary people (supported by Genesis 18:1-19, Joshua 5:13-14, Mark 16:5)
- Believers might encounter angels face-to-face without recognition
- Angels serve as God's messengers and emissaries in ongoing work

**About God's Activity:**
- God actively works in the world through supernatural means
- Divine intervention often occurs without human awareness
- God sends powerful beings to assist believers, demonstrating His care

**Practical Applications:**
- Show hospitality like the Good Samaritan
- Help those in need: elderly, beggars, travelers
- Exercise discretion while maintaining family safety
- Serve to please God rather than seeking recognition

### Ministry to Prisoners (verse 3)
Believers are called to remember and minister to prisoners and the ill-treated. This includes:

**Rationale for Prison Ministry:**
- Demonstrates true religion (James 1:27) - caring for those who can give nothing in return
- Prisoners often prove receptive to the gospel, recognizing their mistakes
- Some prisoners may be unjustly incarcerated, including believers imprisoned for faith
- Effective outreach that transforms both prisoners and those serving

**Methods:**
- Prayer and face-to-face encouragement

- Unnecessary or burdensome

- Easily dissolved

**Biblical Perspective:**

- Trust in God's faithfulness and care

- Using money as a tool for serving God and doing good

- Security in uncertain circumstances

**Hebrews 9-13 Bible Study: Christ Our Sacrifice and Final Applications**

## Structure Overview

**I. Christ our sacrifice (9-14)**
**II. Final applications (15-19)** 
**III. Closing benedictions and greetings (20-25)**

## Key Study Questions and Applications

### Warning Against False Teachings (Verses 8-14)

**Core Principle: Jesus Christ is unchanging** - Verse 8 establishes that Jesus is the same yesterday, today, and forever. Because He is unchanging, truth itself is unchanging. Truth doesn't vary from generation to generation, country to country, church to church, or culture to culture.

**Identifying False Teaching:** There will be no new "revelations" from God that contradict biblical teaching. Cults often build upon strange or new teachings not recognized in Scripture. The second part of verse 9 indicates some groups were emphasizing ritualistic foods or meals, possibly referencing Levitical priests eating sacrificial portions.

**The Altar (Verse 10):** This refers to Christ's sacrifice on the cross - the "altar" believers approach for forgiveness of sins. Priests following the Old Covenant system could not partake of Christ's sacrifice.

**Outside the Gate (Verses 11-12):** References the Day of Atonement practice where the priest's sacrifice blood was taken into the Holy of Holies, while the animal's body was burned outside camp, symbolically removing sins from the congregation. Jesus became the ultimate sacrifice this foreshadowed - offering His own blood once and for all, suffering outside Jerusalem's gate.

**Going Outside the Camp (Verse 13):** The call to "go out to Him outside the camp" uses figurative language. The "camp" may represent popular opinion or worldly thinking that must be abandoned to follow Jesus. This requires "bearing His reproach" - willingness to suffer persecution and scorn for association with Christ.

**Eternal vs. Temporal Focus (Verse 14):** Great cities throughout history - Babylon, Nineveh, Jerusalem, Rome, Xi'an - all rose and fell. Earthly achievements are temporary, but God's kingdom is eternal. Believers face a choice: spend life building God's kingdom or their own through material accumulation, money, security, and position.

### Supporting Scripture References

**Warnings against false teachings:**
- Romans 16:17 - Watch for those causing divisions contrary to learned teaching
- 2 Corinthians 10:4-5 - Divine weapons demolish arguments against God's knowledge  
- Ephesians 4:14 - Avoid being tossed by every wind of teaching
- 2 Timothy 3:16 - Scripture is God-breathed for teaching and correction

**Old Testament foundation:**
- Leviticus 4:21 - Sin offering taken outside camp and burned

## Practical Christian Living (Verses 15-19)

### Continual Praise and Worship

**Sacrifice of Praise (Verse 15):** The word "continually" expands praise beyond weekly church services to encompass all of life. True sacrifice costs something - as David said, he wouldn't offer God something costing him nothing. Believers should sacrifice money, time, personal glory, desires, plans, and "rights" for God.

**Fruit of the Lips:** Praise originates in the heart but should be verbally expressed. Speaking praise clarifies exactly what we're thanking God for - His creation under moonlight, children's personalities, provision of food, His character revealed in Scripture.

### Christian Community and Sharing

**Adult Sharing (Verse 16):** Sharing isn't just for children - all believers should actively share resources, time, encouragement, and hospitality with others. This pleases God and strengthens community bonds.

### Church Leadership and Authority

**Submitting to Leaders (Verse 17):** Clear command to obey spiritual authority like church elders. These leaders "keep watch over souls" - a serious responsibility requiring vigilance and action when sin appears. They will report to God on their stewardship.

**Leadership Applications:**
- Be faithful, vigilant, and diligent
- Actively observe believers and address sin
- Carry out shepherding responsibilities seriously

**Congregation Applications:**
- Submit to legitimate spiritual authority
- Avoid causing grief through division, complaints, gossip, or ignoring teachings
- Be "good sheep" who support rather than undermine leadership

**Prayer for Leaders (Verses 18-19):** Churches improve when congregations pray for leaders instead of complaining about them. Show hospitality, encouragement, and support. Focus on giving rather than getting.

### Supporting Worship Scriptures

- Psalms 54:6 - Freewill offering of praise
- Psalms 100 - Comprehensive call to worship with gladness, thanksgiving, and praise
- 1 Peter 5:5 - Submit to elders with humility

## Closing Benediction and Final Themes

### Christ's Superiority Throughout Hebrews

Key passages demonstrating Christ's supremacy:
- Hebrews 1:2-4 - Superior to prophets and angels
- Hebrews 2:14-15 - Victory over death
- Hebrews 3:3,6 - Greater than Moses  
- Hebrews 4:14-15 - Superior High Priest
- Hebrews 7:26-27 - Perfect sacrifice
- Hebrews 8:1 - Heavenly ministry
- Hebrews 9:14 - Eternal redemption

Repeated exhortations to perseverance:
- Hebrews 2:1 - Pay attention to what was heard
- Hebrews 2:3 - Don't neglect salvation
- Hebrews 3:12-13 - Guard against unbelief
- Hebrews 3:14 - Hold confidence firm to the end

## Discussion Questions for Application

1. **False Teaching Discernment:** How can believers best avoid strange/false teachings? What should the response be to claims of new revelation?

2. **Sacrifice and Commitment:** What specific things are you willing to sacrifice in praise to God? What does "bearing Christ's reproach" look like practically?

3. **Kingdom Building:** Are you building God's kingdom or your own? What daily choices reflect eternal versus temporal priorities?

4. **Church Relationships:** How do you support spiritual leaders? What behaviors cause grief to church leadership?

5. **Continual Worship:** How can praise become truly "continual" beyond Sunday services?

## Key Takeaways

**Doctrinal Foundation:** Jesus Christ's unchanging nature provides stability against false teaching. His once-for-all sacrifice supersedes Old Covenant practices while fulfilling their symbolic meaning.

**Practical Faith:** Christian living involves continual praise, sacrificial giving, mutual sharing, respect for spiritual authority, and prayer support for leaders.

**Eternal Perspective:** Focus on God's eternal kingdom rather than temporary earthly achievements. Be willing to suffer reproach for Christ's sake.

**Community Responsibility:** Believers have obligations both to support leadership and to maintain unity through submission, prayer, and encouragement rather than division and complaint.

The study emphasizes applying Hebrews' principles consistently, asking participants to reflect on growth areas and continued application of learned truths in daily Christian living.
