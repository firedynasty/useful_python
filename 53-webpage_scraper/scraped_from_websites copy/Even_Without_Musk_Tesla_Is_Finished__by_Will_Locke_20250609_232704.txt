# Even Without Musk, Tesla Is Finished
## The market is leapfrogging the deplorable duo.

Remember when we all thought Musk was the real-life Iron Man? A visionary inventor and engineer trailblazing humanity to a brighter future with his vast technological leaps. Well, that facade didn’t last long, did it? His now-open Nazi-adjacent techno-libertarianism ideology has shattered the illusion, and at this point we can all see he is more similar to Marvel’s Hydra than Captain America. But, for some bizarre reason, people still seem to think that Musk is some business and engineering genius, just one who has swung wildly to the right. Well, that isn’t true. Musk is Mysterio, a master illusionist — he’s able to make a pile of st seem appealing and nothing more. There is no greater evidence of this than Tesla. You see, Tesla needs Musk to hide that it is obsolete and on the verge of death.
Tesla is the most overvalued car company on the planet, even after its recent 50%+ share price drop. Why? Investors are heavily speculating that Tesla will lead the EV and autonomous driving revolution by a wide margin and, in doing so, dominate the industry. This is the line Musk has fed the market, and they have accepted it hook, line, and sinker. But, in reality, Tesla is falling behind rapidly.
Say you want an affordable yet useable EV; in the past, the obvious choice was the standard Model 3, but that is no longer the case. The Kia EV3 undercuts the Model 3 by £4,000 ($5,180) while still offering a WLTP range of 376 miles, which is 32 more miles than the Model 3. While the Model 3 does charge slightly faster on paper, in the real world, it doesn’t (Tesla tends to exaggerate these numbers). Moreover, the interior quality and specs of the EV3 are far more luxurious than those of the Model 3, which has a cheap feeling of minimalism thanks to the single screen and bad materials. On top of that, the EV3 seems to hold its value far better and has a longer warranty.

Only a die-hard Musk fan would choose the Tesla over the Kia.
But what if you want to splash out and get a properly long-range EV?
Again, years ago, you’d get a long-range Model 3 or even a Model S if you wanted to really splash out. But that isn’t the case anymore.
Mercedes has just released its CLA 250+, a Model 3-sized EV capable of doing 492 miles per charge, and Mercedes has even stated that it can manage 431 miles per charge if driven exclusively at motorway speeds, due to it being so efficient. For comparison, a Tesla Model 3 LR RWD can only handle 325 miles at highway speeds at best. Tesla’s longest-range EV, the Model S, is only rated at 410 miles on the unrealistic EPA range estimate. Yet, the Mercedes is nearly half the price of the Model S! Meanwhile, the CLA 250+ is only £5,000 ($6,476) more expensive than the Model 3 LR RWD, yet has the design, features, quality, and brand image of a Mercedes, which is several leagues above Tesla.
Again, only a Musk fan would choose the Model 3 or S over the CLA+.
But I hear a few people in the background mumbling something about FSD. Yeah… no. Tesla is losing the autonomous driving race to Mercedes too.
Mercedes has this brilliant system called Drive Pilot that is literally leaps and bounds ahead of Tesla’s so-called Full Self Driving (FSD).
There are two core elements that make Drive Pilot far more reliable than FSD: the backup control systems, which offer several ways to steer and brake, and the redundancy in the sensor suite, where multiple types of sensors are included so that if one type fails, the other can fill in the gaps. Drive Pilot also senses the world with far higher fidelity than FSD, which lightens the load on the AI, as it doesn’t have to interpret what it is seeing. This, combined with proper AI development, means Drive Pilot is a far more capable and accurate system.
As a result, Drive Pilot is legally a Level 3 autonomous system, meaning that in some states and countries, the driver can take their hands off the wheel under certain circumstances.
Meanwhile, FSD is still Level 2, meaning the driver must always have their hands on the steering wheel and, despite their best efforts, can’t get certified as Level 3. This is partially due to the lack of redundant backup systems in FSD and partly because the system is demonstrably dangerous. Tesla’s own figures heavily suggest that FSD has a fatality rate ten times higher than an unassisted human driver! Meanwhile, I can’t find a single reported crash involving Drive Pilot, despite thousands of cars worldwide using the system.
So, no, Tesla does not have an autonomous driving advantage and is, in fact, miles behind the pack, given that it isn’t just Mercedes that has achieved Level 3. This does not bode well for the future of Tesla, as Musk has seemingly pinned its entire future on the Cybercab, a self-driving, steering wheel-less and pedal-less car powered by FSD — a vehicle that experts almost unanimously agree will be wildly dangerous due to FSD’s flaws (read more [here](/predict/we-are-watching-the-death-of-tesla-04639adfdb7b)).
Maybe Tesla doesn’t have a market advantage, and their self-driving technology is effectively useless vapourware at this point. But they make their own battery, the 4680, which is the best and cheapest in the industry. Right?
Nope. Not even close.
BYD just announced their new Super e-Platform, and its battery technology dwarfs anything Tesla has even claimed to have.
This LFP battery has a 1000V charging voltage and a 1000 A charge current, meaning it has a peak charge rate of 1000 kW! That is four times the peak of Tesla’s 4680. But the 4680’s average charge rate is only 124 kW, while BYD’s remained steady at 600 kW until the battery hit 90% charge! That means that in just five minutes of charge time, this battery can add 249 miles of range, which is about how much range a 4680-powered Model Y gets from 20+ minutes of charging.
With specs like this, you might expect the Super e-Platform to be expensive, but it isn’t. Thanks to using LFP battery chemistry, BYD can put an 83.2 kWh version of this battery pack into their upcoming upgraded Han L EV, which will only cost the equivalent of about $37,000 when it comes out. Now, because BYD has an operational profit of 20%, and the battery accounts for roughly 30% of the build cost of an EV, we can estimate that this battery costs $8,880 to produce, giving us a price per kWh of $106 per kWh. That is pretty much exactly how much the 4680 costs to produce (read more [here](/predict/tesla-is-trying-to-fix-its-biggest-mistake-a7663fb6a3ec)), yet the BYD battery has significantly better specs! Moreover, this price could soon plummet due to BYD’s efforts to halve the costs of LFP chemistry.
No matter how you spin it, Tesla is lagging well behind the wider industry. It isn’t a trailblazer set to dominate; it is a business based on hollow hype and vapourware.
This is why Tesla will die even without Elon. If you actually value Tesla in line with other automakers, its value dips to tens of billions of dollars less than its debts and liabilities (read more [here](/predict/this-is-how-tesla-will-die-d0ca25199b9f)). In other words, if you valued Tesla without the Musk hype, it would actually have a negative valuation, which I trust I don’t have to explain why this isn’t good.
As EVs like the CLA+, batteries like BYD’s Super e-Platform, and autonomous systems like Drive Pilot begin to gain traction in the industry, which they are already doing, investors, customers, and Tesla’s lenders will lose interest in Tesla. Sales will slump, valuations will plummet, and eventually, the bailiffs will come knocking, and the Tesla we once knew will be gone forever. Musk isn’t destroying Tesla with his ethically broken politics; Tesla is dying of its own accord. Musk is just speeding up this inevitable process.
