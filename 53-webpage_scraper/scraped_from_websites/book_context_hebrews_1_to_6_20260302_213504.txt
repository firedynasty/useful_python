# Hebrews 1-6: Bible Study Analysis

## Background of the Book

**Authorship**: This is the only New Testament book with unknown authorship. Various candidates have been proposed including Paul, Barnabas, Silas, Peter, and Apollos, but nothing definitive has been concluded. The early church fathers didn't know who wrote this book. The anonymous authorship is fitting since the book focuses entirely on Christ's supremacy above all else. As 2 Peter 1:21 states: "For no prophecy was ever produced by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit."

**Audience**: Written specifically to Hebrew Christians. The book contains no mention of Gentiles or Jew-Gentile conflicts common in other early church writings. It includes extensive Old Testament references familiar to Jewish readers and detailed discussions of priesthood, ceremonial laws, and the Levitical system.

**Central Theme**: The superiority and pre-eminence of Christ over everything and everyone in glory and power. This theme permeates the entire book and has profound implications for how believers should live with Christ on His throne and submit their will to Him.

## Chapter 1: Christ's Supreme Revelation and Nature

### I. Christ is God's Revelation to the World (1:1-4)

**Progressive Revelation**: God has spoken "in many times and in many ways" throughout history. In the Old Testament, He primarily communicated through prophets who prepared the way for Christ by:
- Showing people their lost state and need for divine help
- Demonstrating human inability to reach God through personal effort  
- Providing hundreds of prophecies about the coming Messiah

The entire Old Testament serves as preparation for Christ's coming as the culmination of God's plan, showing both God's grace and human sin while pointing to the need for a Savior.

**Methods of Divine Communication**: God has revealed Himself through visions, dreams, angelic messengers, prophetic experiences, inspired Scripture, and ultimately through His Son Jesus. This progressive revelation addresses humanity's spiritual nature and need for relationship with God.

**The Last Days**: We live in the final phase of God's progressive plan. This provides the privilege of understanding the complete picture more clearly than previous generations, though not everything has been fully revealed or fulfilled.

**Christ as Supreme Messenger**: Jesus serves as God's ultimate mouthpiece and representative. He not only spoke God's words but lived as God, providing a perfect example. The principle "What Would Jesus Do?" remains valid because His actions were 100% perfect and wise in every situation.

### Proofs of Christ's Supremacy (1:2-4)

The author establishes eight foundational proofs of Christ's supremacy:

1. **Heir of all things** - He inherits everything
2. **Creator of the world** - "Through him all things were made; without him nothing was made that has been made" (John 1:3)
3. **Radiance of His glory** - He perfectly reflects God's divine glory
4. **Exact representation of His nature** - He embodies God's essence completely
5. **Sustainer of all things** - "He upholds all things by the word of His power"
6. **Purifier of sins** - He accomplished redemption
7. **Seated at God's right hand** - He holds the position of supreme authority
8. **Superior to angels** - He ranks above the highest created beings

### II. Christ Exalted Above Angels (1:5-14)

**Why Angels Matter**: Angels possess extraordinary power and serve crucial roles in God's plan. Biblical examples include one angel killing 185,000 soldiers in a single night (2 Kings 19:35) and the destroying angel in Egypt. Angels appear in God's throne room, fight spiritual battles, protect believers, and answer prayers. They often inspire fear and worship when they appear to humans.

**Systematic Proof of Superiority**: The author uses Old Testament quotations to systematically prove Christ's superiority:

**Verse 5 - Unique Sonship**: 
- Psalms 2:7: "You are my Son; today I have begotten you"
- 2 Samuel 7:14: "I will be his father, and he will be my son"
Angels are created beings without this Father-Son relationship with God.

**Verse 6 - Worship**: 
Angels are commanded to worship the Messiah, placing them in a subordinate position.

**Verse 7 - Different Nature**: 
Angels serve as "winds" and "flaming fire" (Psalms 104:4), showing their role as servants rather than rulers.

**Verses 8-9 - Eternal Kingship**: 
Quoting Psalms 45:6-7, the text establishes Christ as eternal King with characteristics of:
- Forever throne and dominion
- Perfect righteousness 
- Hatred of sin
- Divine anointing with gladness

**Verses 10-12 - Creator and Sustainer**: 
Christ created the world, demonstrating His supreme power and wisdom. The complexity and beauty of creation - beaches, forests, mountains, starlit skies, coral reefs, crystal lakes - testify to infinite wisdom and power that could never result from random chance. 

More significantly, Christ will one day destroy and recreate the world (2 Peter 3:10-12, Revelation 21:1). While everything visible is temporary, Christ remains eternally unchanged. He existed before creation and will continue unchanged after this world passes away.

**Verse 13 - Position of Authority**: 
Christ sits at God's right hand - the supreme position of power and authority. No angel holds this position.

**Verse 14 - Angelic Service**: 
Angels are "ministering spirits sent out to serve" believers, demonstrating their servant role rather than ruling position. This also reveals God's love in providing angelic assistance to His people.

## Key Applications and Life Impact

**Recognizing Christ's Supremacy**: Understanding Christ's supreme position should fundamentally transform how believers:
- Prioritize their lives with Christ at the center
- Submit their will to His authority
- Approach Scripture study and obedience
- Respond to difficult circumstances
- Make daily decisions using the "What Would Jesus Do?" principle

**Response to Progressive Revelation**: Since God has revealed Himself through His Word, believers must:
- Study Scripture diligently to understand this revelation
- Obey what has been revealed
- Trust God's timing for understanding unclear matters
- Accept that some questions await face-to-face encounter with Christ

**Attitude Toward Sin**: Following Christ's example of loving righteousness and hating sin should shape believers' moral responses and lifestyle choices.

**Worship and Perspective**: Recognizing that even powerful angels worship Christ should inspire proper worship attitudes and help maintain eternal perspective amid temporary earthly concerns.

**Trust in Christ's Sustaining Power**: Understanding that Christ "upholds all things by the word of His power" provides security and confidence in His ability to sustain believers through any circumstance.

The systematic presentation in Hebrews 1 establishes the foundation for the entire book's argument: if Christ is superior to the most powerful created beings (angels), then He is indeed supreme over all creation and deserving of absolute devotion and obedience.

# Hebrews Chapters 1-6: Bible Study Analysis

## Chapter Structure and Key Themes

**Chapter 1-2 Overview:**
I. The salvation we have in Christ (1-4)
II. Christ subjected Himself, but will be exalted (5-9) 
III. Christ came to bring us into His family (10-18)

## Chapter 2: Detailed Analysis

### The Supremacy of Christ's Message (Verses 1-4)

**Key Principle: Avoiding Spiritual Drift**
The author uses the word "drift" deliberately - it represents a slow, often unintentional change in direction. Just as boats drift off course without careful navigation, believers can gradually drift away from God's truth. This drift is incremental and subtle:

- First, postponing quiet time ("I'll read the Bible tomorrow")
- Then unconfessed sin accumulating
- Resentment building toward God's allowances in life
- Skipping church occasionally, then regularly
- Eventually abandoning Bible reading and prayer entirely

**The Greater/Lesser Argument:**
If disobeying messages delivered by angels brought serious consequences, how much more serious are the consequences of rejecting salvation offered by Christ Himself? Angels were instrumental in bringing Scripture to God's people through visions, dreams, and direct messages to prophets like Abraham, Daniel, Mary, and Joseph.

**Confirmation of the Gospel:**
The salvation message was:
1. First spoken by Christ
2. Confirmed by His apostles and disciples
3. Verified through signs, wonders, and miracles
4. Passed down through generations

God used miraculous signs to verify the gospel when the early church was growing, helping people distinguish truth from passing religious fads.

### Christ's Humiliation and Exaltation (Verses 5-9)

**Christ's Temporary Lowering:**
Christ became lower than angels for a short period by taking human form. This represented an enormous sacrifice:
- Left paradise for a sin-cursed earth
- Born in a manger rather than a palace
- Came as servant, not earthly king
- Gave His life as ransom for many

**The Principle of Humility and Exaltation:**
God established the principle of exalting those who humble themselves (1 Peter 5:6). Christ exemplifies this perfectly - He humbled Himself more than anyone in history and will be exalted higher than anyone in history as King of Kings and Lord of Lords (Philippians 2:12).

**Practical Applications of Humility:**
- Emptying trash and cleaning restrooms
- Getting up for crying babies at night
- Not needing the last word in arguments
- Forgiving repeated mistakes
- Doing unwanted tasks
- Serving without seeking reward

### Christ as Our Brother (Verses 10-18)

**God's Purpose in Creation:**
Verse 10 reveals that everything was made by Him and for Him - the world exists to give God pleasure and glory. Humanity's chief end is to glorify God and enjoy Him fully.

**Christ's Perfection Through Suffering:**
Since Christ's divine nature was already perfect, His perfection through suffering refers to His human nature being perfected through obedience, enabling Him to be:
- An understanding High Priest
- A perfect example for believers
- The establisher of perfect righteousness

**Brotherhood with Christ:**
Believers become Christ's brothers and sisters through adoption into God's family. We share the same Father, fulfilling Old Testament prophecies about the Messiah preaching to His brethren. Jesus confirmed this relationship, saying those who obey God are His brothers and mother (Matthew 12:50, Luke 8:21).

**The Incarnation's Purpose:**
Christ became human for several crucial reasons:

1. **To Defeat Death:** By taking human nature, Christ could die and resurrect, defeating Satan's seeming victory at the Fall. Death no longer has the final word for believers.

2. **To Serve as High Priest:** Only as a human could Christ represent humanity before God. Like Adam brought sin to the human race, Christ as the second Adam brought salvation.

3. **To Experience Temptation:** By becoming human, Christ experienced temptation firsthand, allowing Him to:
   - Provide a perfect example of response to temptation
   - Empathize deeply with human struggles
   - Intercede more personally for believers
   - "Perfect" His human nature through obedient suffering

**Victory Over Satan and Death:**
Christ rendered Satan powerless by conquering death through His resurrection. What Satan thought was his greatest victory - killing the Son of God - became his bitter defeat when Jesus rose from the dead.

**Practical Impact on Fear of Death:**
Because Christ defeated death, believers need not fear it. Death is not the end but has been "swallowed up in victory" (1 Corinthians 15:54). This truth should affect how believers live before death, providing courage and peace in facing mortality.

## Cross-References and Supporting Scriptures

**Key Supporting Passages:**
- Psalm 8:4-6: Foundation for Christ's temporary lowering below angels
- Romans 5:6-8: Christ's death for sinners while we were still powerless
- Philippians 2:2-11: The pattern of humility leading to exaltation
- 1 Corinthians 15:20-28, 54: Christ's conquest over death
- John 1:14: The Word becoming flesh
- Galatians 2:20: Living by faith in the crucified and risen Christ

**Principles of Discipleship:**
Referenced in 1 Timothy 2:5-7, 1 Corinthians 15:5-7, and 2 Timothy 2:2, showing the multiplication and reproduction aspect of passing on the gospel message from generation to generation.

**Spiritual Laws:**
The text establishes that "sin must be punished" - a fundamental spiritual law. Because this is true, either humans face punishment for sin, or Jesus faces it on their behalf. This necessity explains why Christ had to die for humanity's salvation.

## Theological Implications

**Propitiation:** Christ's sacrifice satisfied God's wrath against sin, making salvation possible for all who believe.

**Sanctification:** The process by which believers are made holy, referenced in supporting passages like 2 Timothy 2:21 and 1 Thessalonians 5:23.

**Adoption:** Believers are adopted into God's family, becoming brothers and sisters of Christ and children of the heavenly Father.

This study emphasizes the absolute supremacy of Christ over angels, His willing humiliation for human salvation, and His victory over sin and death, providing both theological foundation and practical application for Christian living.

# Hebrews Bible Study: Chapters 1-6

## Structure Overview

**I. Jesus is superior to Moses (1-6)**
**II. Do not reject God like in the past (7-11)**  
**III. Stand firm in obedience and faith (12-18)**

## Chapter 3:1-6 - Jesus Superior to Moses

### Key Study Questions
- Why are the brethren called "holy"? Are you holy?
- What is our heavenly calling?
- Why is Jesus referred to as the Apostle? What does "apostle" mean?
- Why is He referred to as a High Priest? What things did He and does He do as a Priest?
- Who appointed Him (from verse 2)?
- What comparisons and contrasts are made between Jesus and Moses?
- Why did the author compare these two figures?
- What is the conclusion?
- Explain verse 6. What does it mean that we are part of the house of Christ?
- What signs will show whether or not we are really part of His house?

### Verse by Verse Commentary

**1. Holy brethren** – God has called all believers to be holy.

1 Peter 1:15-16 states: "But as he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct, since it is written, 'You shall be holy, for I am holy.'"

This is God's plan for us. He desires us to be 100% holy all the time. On this earth because of our old, sinful flesh we struggle for this holiness. Paul himself was in a constant tug of war between sin and righteousness (Romans 7). Holiness basically means to be separate (see Leviticus 20:26). We are called to be separate from the world and from sin. We are to be different, set apart.

Are you different? Can people notice something different or even "weird" about you? If not, that is probably because you are not different and have not separated yourself from the world's ways as you should.

Though we are in a constant struggle for holiness, it is something that we cannot achieve on this earth. Through the process of sanctification and consciously putting off and repenting of our sin, we can get nearer this target, but will never reach it until we face God and He finally wipes away all our sin. Yet the author still calls believers holy. Why?

In God's sight that is what we are. When God looks at us, He sees not our own sins, but the righteousness of Christ who swapped Himself for us. When Christ looks at us, He actually replaces our sins and clothes us with His righteousness.

**2. Partakers of a heavenly calling** – We are citizens of heaven and ambassadors of Christ. Our identity is not to be rooted in our nationality, tax bracket, or club we belong to. It is not in our favorite sports team, our gender, or our vocation. Our core identity is rooted in the fact that we are Christians, followers of Christ. We serve God's kingdom, the kingdom of heaven over and above every earthly pursuit. 

God calls us to holiness. He calls us to be a light in the world. He calls us to share the good news with everyone, everywhere. He calls us to live our lives worthily as His soldiers, His messengers, even His family members.

We are allowed to join this cause not just in the low position of an unpaid volunteer, but actually as a family member of the boss. We are children of God and are called to represent our Father to the world.

**3. Apostle** – An apostle means "sent one". Jesus was sent by the Father as He Himself attested to many times.

**4. High Priest** – A major theme of chapters 4-10 will be Jesus as our High Priest. A priest is someone who goes in between people and God, like a mediator. Jesus performed this task, bridging the gap between us and God.

**5. Jesus is superior to Moses.** Remember from chapter 1 that a major theme of Hebrews is Jesus' superiority and pre-eminence. The writer spent much of chapters 1-2 proving that Jesus is superior to the angels. Now in verses 1-6, he shows that Jesus is superior Moses.

How did the Jews view Moses? Moses was the most respected figure in all of Jewish history. It was through Moses that God freed His people from Egypt. It was through Moses that God performed many awesome wonders that became known around the world. It was through Moses that He gave the people the Ten Commandments and the entire Torah (the first 5 books of the Bible). Moses was raised up so high in Jewish culture that it was actually a problem where people were almost becoming followers of Moses instead of followers of God.

By showing that Jesus was superior to Moses, the author could show that He is indeed the Messiah and therefore worthy of praise and obedience. He gives several comparisons and contrasts between Jesus and Moses:

A. Jesus and Moses were both faithful to do all of the tasks that God gave them.

B. Jesus should receive more glory because He built the house (God's kingdom), while Moses was a servant in the house.

C. Moses was a servant in God's house, while Christ was actually God's Son. Who has a higher position, Son or servant?

**6. We are this house.** We are the body of Christ. Christ has brought us by adoption into the very household of God. Signs of being in this house include holding fast and keeping firm in our hope until the end. Some may appear for a while to be part of the house, but then fall away. True members of God's family will never fall away.

## Chapter 3:7-11 - Warning Against Hardening Hearts

### Key Study Questions
- Why does the author say that this passage (from Psalms) was authored by the Holy Spirit? What does this tell us about the authority and inspiration of Scripture?
- What does it mean to "harden your heart"?
- In what way did they provoke God?
- How were they testing God?
- What kind of "works" or miracles did they see that God did for the forty years they were in the wilderness? Did these convince them? Why or why not?
- Why could they not enter into the Promised Land (My rest)? What does this tell us about God's nature and the consequences of sin?

### Verse by Verse Commentary

**1. Just as the Holy Spirit says** – This passage is quoted from Psalms 95. Scholars are not 100% sure who authored it, but many believe David did. Yet here the writer ascribes these words to the Holy Spirit. Why? It doesn't matter who the human author is. All of Scripture is inspired by God. The Holy Spirit moved people to write down God's message. The human writer was the tool the Holy Spirit used to bring God's message to the people.

This is another reminder that all of Scripture is God's words and not mere human's own opinions. In the beginning of chapter one we noted that we cannot comprehend God on our own. Only if He reveals Himself to us, can we understand His character and plan. Because He did reveal Himself to us and has given us His message, we must PAY ATTENTION to it and take the time to diligently study it!

**2. The author first quotes from Psalms 95** about the failures of the Israelites in the Old Testament and then will go on to explain these verses making points and applications from them. Pastor John MacArthur says that he makes a 3-point exposition of this passage: firstly, "beware of unbelief" (12-19); secondly, "be afraid of falling short" (4:1-10); thirdly, "be diligent to enter" (4:11-13).

**3. The 40 years in the wilderness** teaches us about people and about God. What consequences did they face for their unbelief and disobedience?

## Chapter 3:12-19 - Beware of Unbelief

### Key Study Questions
- In what ways might an unbelieving heart manifest itself?
- How can we increase our faith in God?
- What can we do to help others not fall away?
- What specific things can you do to encourage others?
- In what ways would you like other people to encourage you?
- What does it mean that we are partakers of Christ?
- Why do you think the author talks so many times about holding fast?
- What provokes God?
- What happened to the people who provoked God?

### Verse by Verse Commentary

**1. Take care not to have an evil, unbelieving heart** – The first two words of this verse are very important. We should be mindful of every area of our Christian life. That means we should watch ourselves. We should continually evaluate ourselves. If we have any sins or sinful tendencies, we must confess them quickly before they can grow and spread. Sin is like a cancer and must be cut out mercilessly.

We learn in Hebrews 2:1 that it can be easy to drift away from God. Believers who are walking faithfully with God wouldn't typically wake up one day and then decide to commit heinous sins. It is often a gradual process.

When can unbelief start to sneak in?

A. **Not getting what we want in prayer** – We approach God with a demand in prayer. We do not receive it and grow upset that God hasn't given us what we want. Doubts may start to creep in.

B. **Facing a difficult trial** – When facing a trial, some would begin to doubt God's goodness and/or sovereignty. Any trials can cause a person to begin to grow bitter against God including the death of a loved one, loss of a job, or a painful illness. Once a bitter spirit is tolerated the next step is a complete falling away from the Lord.

C. **Not keeping a close relationship with Him** – If we are just doing the actions without renewing our mind and heart, unbelief can creep in.

**2. We have a responsibility to help other brothers and sisters not to fall away from God.** God has called us to encourage others daily. This is something we should be doing regularly as a lifestyle, not just now and then. God knows our heart's inclination toward sin. He knows the struggles we face and He knows that if we are on our own, the likelihood that we will fall into sin is much greater. In His great love and mercy, He has provided a large Christian family for us.

What are some specific things we can do to encourage others in their relationship with Christ?

A. **Phone calls** – Sometimes just seeing a person one time a week during Bible study or church is not enough. Picking up the phone is a great way to learn more about the real-life issues others are facing as well as how we can pray for and help them.

B. **Face-to-face meetings** – Invite people to your home for meals. Invite your friends out for tea or a meal. Invite other believers out to do sports or exercise together.

C. **Emails, letters or cards** – Physical cards can be a great encouragement.

D. **Text messages** – One of the simplest ways to encourage others is through a simple text message to show each other we care.

E. **During these meetings go deeper in your conversations.** Share what you have been learning in the Word. Share prayer requests. Share struggles and victories. Don't just talk about the weather and how busy you are at work.

**3. We are partakers of Christ** – Jesus said that His disciples needed to actually eat His flesh. This was an illustration for the total commitment we need and the fact that we must totally rely on Him. We cannot be His disciple unless we completely identify with Him, making Him the Lord of our life. It is not acceptable to live our life the same way we did in the past while adding in a 20-minute praise session per week. We need to imitate His character and learn from His example.

**4. Provoking God** – Sometimes we think of God as a distant being without much contact with or care about what is going on in the world. When we view God like this, we would be tempted to think that He doesn't care much about our sins. We need to realize that our sin provokes God. It is grossly offensive to Him.

**5. The consequences of sin** – Don't be fooled. God sees our sin. How does He react to it? Verse 17 shows us that it makes Him angry. This is the righteous anger which flows out of His holy nature.

Example: the people who provoked God with their sins in the wilderness wandered in it for forty years before dying without ever seeing the Promised Land. Their sin came with a high cost.

**6. Warning about God's rest** – In these verses the implication is that just as those people who provoked God could not enter into the land of rest (the Promised Land), if we follow their example in provoking God, we could not enter into His rest (heaven) either. Don't think that our fate will be different than theirs. If you have any sinful habits, know that continuing in them is just provoking God. He sees and we will also face the consequences.

## Key Themes and Applications

1. **Jesus' Supremacy** - The author establishes Jesus as superior to Moses, the most revered figure in Jewish history, demonstrating His worthiness of complete devotion and obedience.

2. **The Call to Holiness** - Believers are called "holy brethren" not because of their perfect behavior, but because God sees them clothed in Christ's righteousness while calling them to live differently from the world.

3. **Heavenly Identity** - Christians are "partakers of a heavenly calling," meaning their primary identity and loyalty should be to God's kingdom rather than earthly affiliations.

4. **Warning Against Unbelief** - Using the Israelites' wilderness experience as a cautionary example, the author warns against the gradual drift into unbelief that can result from unanswered prayers, trials, or spiritual complacency.

5. **Daily Encouragement** - Believers have a responsibility to encourage one another daily through phone calls, face-to-face meetings, and deeper spiritual conversations to prevent falling away.

6. **The Deceitfulness of Sin** - Sin gradually deceives and hardens hearts, requiring constant vigilance and immediate confession to prevent spiritual drift.

7. **God's Righteous Anger** - God is not distant or indifferent to sin; He is provoked by disobedience, as demonstrated by the consequences faced by the wilderness generation who died without entering the Promised Land.

# Hebrews 4:1-10 Bible Study: The Promise of Rest

## Key Structure and Themes

**Main Outline:**
I. A promise of rest remains (verse 1)
II. We must believe if we want to enter (verses 2-3)
III. This rest is a finished work God has completed (verses 4-5)
IV. This invitation is still open (verses 6-9)

**Central Questions:**
• What is the "therefore" there for?
• Does the promise still remain of entering His rest?
• What does His rest refer to?

## Verse by Verse Analysis

### The Foundation of Fear and Respect (Verse 1)

The "therefore" connects to the previous passage showing the result for rebellious people in the wilderness who did not obey God or believe in Him. They came short of God's rest and instead hardened their hearts repeatedly. God records history in the Bible so we can learn from others' mistakes and not repeat the same sins.

The first step is to fear God. As Proverbs states, the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom. We should respect God and fear the results of willful rebellion against Him and rejection of the gospel. However, the author is not teaching that real Christians need constant fear of losing salvation or a lifestyle of fear. Rather, we should fear IF we fall short of this rest.

**Key Cross-References for Spiritual Evaluation:**
- 1 Corinthians 10:12 – "So, if you think you are standing firm, be careful that you don't fall!"
- 2 Corinthians 13:5 – "Examine yourselves to see whether you are in the faith; test yourselves."
- Philippians 2:12 – "Continue to work out your salvation with fear and trembling."

### Faith as the Basis for Entry (Verses 2-3)

**Knowledge vs. Faith:** The Israelites in the wilderness had extensive knowledge. Moses taught them regularly about God, gave them the Law and divine revelations, and they witnessed miracles. They knew everything needed, but this knowledge did not profit them because it was not acted upon by faith.

For example, God promised them the Promised Land and victory. If they believed, they would have obeyed and taken possession. Instead, they disbelieved and feared the people. The lesson: **Knowledge without faith (and resulting action) is useless.**

The Hebrew readers knew much about Christ, but some remained undecided about believing. The gospel message preached to them would not help unless they chose faith in Christ.

**Application for Bible Study:** If the only accomplishment is gaining Bible knowledge, studies become pointless. Knowledge must be BELIEVED, OBEYED, and APPLIED.

**What "Rest" Means:** For the Israelites, it was physical rest in the Promised Land with peace from enemies. Since God has not promised that land to every believer (only Jews), the rest here refers to eternal life. This rest will be fully realized after this life, free from toil, temptations, persecutions, suffering, and pain. However, eternal life begins now - John 5:24 uses present tense: "whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life."

### God's Finished Work (Verses 4-5)

**Completed from Foundation:** MacArthur explains, "The spiritual rest which God gives is not something incomplete or unfinished. It is a rest based upon a finished work which God purposed in eternity past, just like the rest which God took after He finished creation."

**God's Rest After Creation:** God's rest after creation was not from tiredness but demonstrated completion of His work. He established a precedent that after completed work comes rest. In Old Testament law, people worked six days and rested on the seventh, following God's model. Every seventh year was jubilee - fields lay fallow and servants were released.

**Modern Application:** Like preparing for an exam with one book left to read, or having an unfinished work project - true rest requires completion of urgent tasks. We cannot enter permanent rest with Christ until our earthly work is finished. Ephesians 2:10 states we are "created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them." God has prepared specific works for each believer to accomplish.

### The Open Invitation (Verses 6-9)

**Still Available:** Despite many people failing to enter through disobedience in the past, the opportunity remains open. The writer attributes Psalm 95 to David, who quoted it as an invitation that "today" people could still accept. The invitation remains open - there still remains a Sabbath rest for God's people.

**The Big Picture Message:**
A. Those who don't believe won't enter that rest
B. Those who believe can enter that rest  
C. Believe NOW so you don't miss it

**Biblical Urgency:** Throughout Scripture are invitations to repent and turn to God. Jesus said, "Repent for the kingdom of heaven is at hand" (Matthew 4:17). Isaiah and Paul emphasized this urgency. The salvation message should not be delayed.

**Supporting Verses:**
- Isaiah 55:6 – "Seek the Lord while he may be found; call on him while he is near."
- 2 Corinthians 6:2 – "Now is the time of God's favor, now is the day of salvation."
- Romans 10:17 – "Faith comes from hearing the message, and the message is heard through the word about Christ."
- Matthew 7:21 – "Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father who is in heaven."

**Noah's Example:** Noah preached righteousness for over a hundred years with no listeners. When the flood came, people likely changed their hearts, but it was too late - the door was shut and opportunity was gone. The ark could have held more people, but they scoffed and wouldn't listen.

## Practical Applications

**Personal Evaluation:**
1. Make sure you have believed the message - evaluate your spiritual standing regularly
2. Share the gospel urgently with people around you
3. Don't put off sharing with family and friends - plead with them to turn to God while they have opportunity

**Discussion Questions:**
• What similarities exist between us and the Israelites in the wilderness?
• Why was knowledge not helpful for them?
• Under what condition is knowledge helpful?
• What works were finished from the foundation of the world?
• Why did God rest after creation, and how does this relate to believers' rest?
• Why could the people originally offered this "rest" not enter?
• What specific action do you need to take this week to apply these lessons?

The central message emphasizes that God's rest - eternal life - remains available through faith, not works or knowledge alone. The invitation is urgent and present, requiring immediate response while opportunity exists.

# Hebrews 4:11-16 Bible Study

This study examines a pivotal passage in Hebrews that bridges the theme of entering God's rest with the introduction of Christ as our perfect High Priest.

## Main Outline

**I. Be diligent to enter the rest (v.11)**
**II. We are accountable to follow God's Word (v.12-13)**  
**III. Christ is our perfect High Priest (v.14-16)**

## Key Themes and Applications

### Entering God's Rest (Verse 11)

The passage builds on the theme starting in Hebrews 3:7, where the good news was preached to the Israelites in the wilderness who hardened their hearts and provoked God. The same good news is preached to believers today, with even greater promises extended to us than to them.

**Application:** We must be diligent to enter into God's rest and not fall short. This requires regularly evaluating our spiritual position, holding fast to God's Word in faith, and not allowing ourselves to drift away from God. We must learn from the example of those who rebelled in the wilderness and avoid their mistakes.

### The Living Word of God (Verse 12)

Verse 12 is one of the most famous verses about God's Word: "For the word of God is living and active, sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing to the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow, and discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart."

**Key Insights:**
- The Bible is not "just an old book" but is alive and active
- It brings life to those who obey it and transforms lives
- It penetrates people's hearts with power to change lives for eternity
- When we hear God's Word, a response is demanded - we must obey
- The Bible contains every moral principle needed for life and godliness

**Supporting Scripture:** Isaiah 55:11 states God's word "will not return to me empty, but will accomplish what I desire and achieve the purpose for which I sent it."

### God's Omniscience (Verse 13)

God sees everything we do - there is no secret we can keep from Him. He is omniscient and omnipresent. This reality should motivate obedience and reverence.

### Christ as Our High Priest (Verses 14-16)

**Jesus as Both Human and Divine:** The passage presents Jesus with His human name and divine title "Son of God," showing both His human and divine natures.

**Christ's Temptations:** Verse 15 reveals that Christ "has been tempted in every way, just as we are—yet he did not sin." This destroys common arguments people make when facing temptation:
- "God doesn't understand what I'm facing"
- "No one could bear up under this temptation" 
- "It's easy for God to tell us not to sin when He hasn't been tempted"

**Christ's Understanding:** Because Jesus experienced the same temptations without sinning, He can sympathize with our weaknesses. His example should motivate us to resist temptation and shows us that victory is possible.

**Access to Grace:** Verse 16 provides the practical application: "Let us then approach God's throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace to help us in time of need."

## Practical Applications

**Prayer in Temptation:** When facing temptation, we should turn to Christ for help. Many times, the "way out" promised in 1 Corinthians 10:13 begins with turning to God in prayer.

**Free Access to God's Throne:** Unlike earthly kings who are guarded and dangerous to approach, the King of Kings gives free access to His throne. Even Queen Esther risked her life entering the king's presence, but believers have a "free lifetime pass" into God's presence.

**Mercy and Grace:** When we sin, Christ offers forgiveness and restoration through confession. His mercy and grace are available in our time of need.

## Discussion Questions

- How would you explain to someone why Christians follow "such an old book"?
- What does it mean that God's Word is "living" and "active"?
- How is God's Word like a sword, and how can we use it as a weapon?
- How does knowing that Christ faced the same temptations help us practically?
- What forms do God's mercy and grace take in our daily lives?

The study emphasizes that this passage demands action - not just intellectual appreciation of Scripture's power, but obedient response to God's Word and confident approach to His throne for help in our spiritual journey.

# Hebrews 5:1-6 Bible Study

## Structure and Themes

This passage presents three main sections examining Christ's role as high priest:

I. The role of the high priest (verses 1-3)
II. The appointing of the high priest (verse 4) 
III. Christ was appointed as high priest by God Himself (verses 5-6)

The central theme connects to Hebrews' overarching message of Christ's supremacy, demonstrating that Jesus fulfills and surpasses the Old Testament priesthood.

## The Role of the High Priest (Verses 1-3)

### Key Functions and Characteristics

**1. Service "On Behalf of Men"**
The high priest's position exists entirely for others' benefit, not personal gain. As the commentary explains: "His position of leadership is used not for himself. It is not used for his own benefit, career, or finances. His job is truly 'for the people.'" This contrasts with leaders who wrongly exploit their positions, whom Jesus warned against as "wolves in sheep's clothes."

**2. Focus on "Things Pertaining to God"** 
The priesthood is fundamentally spiritual. When people sought to make Jesus an earthly king, He refused. As He told Pilate in John 18:33-36, His kingdom was not of this world. The high priest's focus remains on helping God's people grow closer to God, serving as "citizens of and ambassadors of heaven."

This creates tension for ministers who must balance spiritual and physical needs. The study notes: "Helping the physical issues should be a means to an end of helping these people spiritually." It references a Chinese proverb: "Give a man a fish and he will not be hungry for a day. Teach him how to fish and he can feed a village," adding: "Teach him about God who made the fish and he need never be hungry (spiritually) again."

**3. Offering Gifts and Sacrifices for Sins**
The high priest serves as mediator between sinful humans and holy God. He offers daily sacrifices and annually enters the Holy of Holies for atonement. As Hebrews 9:22 states: "without shedding of blood there is no remission." The gifts may refer to voluntary grain offerings showing gratitude or serve as synonymous with sacrifices.

### The High Priest's Humanity (Verse 2)

A crucial aspect is that the high priest comes from among the people, sharing their weaknesses. This enables compassionate ministry rather than harsh judgment. The study emphasizes: "His own weaknesses and sins and mistakes enabled him to treat others with mercy."

**Application for Believers:**
"Remember that we are all sinners. We are not better than any of the people we share the gospel with... All of us are sinners and all of us need God's forgiveness." When tempted to judge others, believers should examine themselves first, following Jesus' teaching about removing the plank from one's own eye before addressing another's speck (Matthew 7:3-5).

**Personal Responsibility in Ministry:**
The high priest offers sacrifices "as for the people, as for himself" (verse 3). Christian leaders must maintain their own relationship with God while serving others. "Every sermon we preach should be a sermon preached to ourselves first and foremost. Every prayer offered for wisdom, forgiveness, and grace for others should be offered for ourselves."

## Divine Appointment (Verse 4)

### God's Sovereign Choice

The priesthood cannot be pursued as a career path. As stated: "A person could not apply for the position and seek it out. One could only be chosen for it, just like God chose Aaron as the first high priest." This divine appointment carries significant implications:

**For the High Priest:**
- Challenges him to excellence, knowing God chose him
- Promotes humility, recognizing it wasn't personal ability
- Encourages dependence on God
- Motivates service for God's glory rather than human approval

**For the People:**
- Provides God's stamp of approval on the high priest
- Demands respect and obedience to rightful spiritual authority
- Establishes authority even kings cannot usurp (2 Chronicles 26:19)

**Modern Applications:**
- Ministry should not be considered merely another career option
- Believers should serve faithfully in whatever God calls them to
- Church members should respect and obey God-appointed leaders "in such a way that can give them joy and not grief"

## Christ's Superior Priesthood (Verses 5-6)

### Divine Appointment of Christ

Verse 5 connects Christ to the established principles of priesthood. Jesus was appointed by God the Father (Luke 9:35), demonstrating the humility and submission seen throughout the Gospels as He came to obey His Father's will (John 4:34).

### The Order of Melchizedek

The passage introduces a crucial concept developed further in Hebrews 7. Christ serves as high priest "according to the order of Melchizedek" rather than Aaron's line. This distinction proves significant:

**Aaron's Priesthood:** Priests only, separate from kingship
**David's Line:** Kings only, separate from priesthood  
**Melchizedek's Order:** Priest-king combination

From Genesis 14:18-20, Melchizedek appears as "king of Salem" who "was priest of God Most High," blessing Abraham and receiving tithes. This dual role of priest-king perfectly describes Christ's office, "reaffirming once again His supremacy."

The study quotes two key Old Testament passages that establish this appointment:
- Psalm 2:7 (regarding Christ as Son)
- Psalm 110:4 (regarding the Melchizedek priesthood)

## Cross-References and Supporting Scriptures

The study provides extensive biblical support:

**Priestly Consecration:** Exodus 28, Numbers 16
**Atonement Practices:** Leviticus 16, Exodus 30:10
**Human Sinfulness:** Romans 3:23, Matthew 7:3-5
**Worldly vs. Heavenly Focus:** 1 John 2:15-17, Colossians 3:2
**Divine Appointment Examples:** 1 Samuel 2:34-35
**Melchizedek Background:** Genesis 14:18-20
**Messianic Psalms:** Psalm 2:7, 110:4

## Key Discussion Questions

The study poses penetrating questions for reflection:
- What is the role of high priest and his attitude toward those he serves?
- How should remembering our own sins affect how we treat others?
- How does one become high priest - through personal ambition or divine calling?
- Why is it important that Christ didn't choose this role for Himself?
- How does God appointing Christ as high priest demonstrate His supremacy?
- What does it mean that Christ serves according to Melchizedek's order?

## Practical Applications

**For Individual Believers:**
- Maintain humility by remembering personal sinfulness when dealing with others
- Resist judgment and criticism, following Jesus' teaching about planks and specks
- Recognize that all service opportunities come from God's calling, not personal ambition
- Balance spiritual and physical ministry, using physical help as means to spiritual ends

**For Church Leaders:**
- Serve for others' benefit, not personal gain
- Focus on spiritual growth and God's kingdom priorities
- Maintain personal relationship with God while serving others
- Remember divine appointment brings both privilege and responsibility
- Practice mercy and patience, remembering shared human weakness

**For Church Members:**
- Respect and support God-appointed leadership
- Avoid treating ministry as merely another career option
- Understand the mediatorial role of spiritual leadership
- Appreciate the sacrifice and service of those in spiritual authority

The study concludes by connecting Christ's priesthood to Hebrews' central theme of His supremacy, showing how Jesus perfectly fulfills and transcends the Old Testament priestly system through His unique appointment as priest-king in Melchizedek's order.

# Hebrews 1-6 Bible Study: Christ's Supremacy and Spiritual Maturity

This Bible study examines key themes in Hebrews 1-6, focusing on Christ's role as high priest and the necessity of spiritual growth and maturity among believers.

## I. Christ's Life as Our High Priest (Hebrews 5:7-10)

### Christ's Humanity and Suffering

Verses 7-10 emphasize Jesus' humanity as essential to His role as high priest. Christ suffered, cried, and prayed for deliverance from the cross, demonstrating He came not as a superhuman who couldn't feel pain or understand human emotions. Because of His humanity, He can represent us before God as one of us, having personally experienced the breadth of human emotions and suffering.

**Key Biblical Cross-References:**
- 2 Corinthians 1:3-5: God comforts us in troubles so we can comfort others
- Matthew 26:38-46: Jesus in Gethsemane preparing for suffering
- Matthew 27:46: Jesus' cry of abandonment on the cross
- 1 Peter 3:18: Christ suffered for the unrighteous to bring us to God

### Understanding Christ's "Learning" and "Perfection"

**Verse 7** likely refers to Jesus' prayer in the Garden of Gethsemane when He asked for the cup to be removed. This request was real and shows Jesus' human side that wanted to avoid the immense pain of the cross. The phrase "in the days of His flesh" implies Jesus existed before His human incarnation, reminding us of His eternal existence.

**Christ's perfection through suffering** (referencing Hebrews 2:10) does not refer to His divine character becoming morally better, nor does it mean He was sinful and became perfect. Rather, His sufferings perfected His human nature by giving Him personal understanding of what we face, enabling deeper sympathy because of His own experiences.

His earthly experiences "perfected" Him as high priest by:
- Proving He was sinless through successfully resisting every temptation
- Fulfilling all righteousness
- Setting the perfect example for us to follow
- Demonstrating things we could not have seen unless Christ became human

**Practical Application:** True belief brings obedience. Followers of Christ are required to obey Him, as faith without actions is dead.

## II. The Need to Grow in Maturity (Hebrews 5:11-14)

### The Problem of Spiritual Immaturity

The author addresses believers who had become "dull of hearing" - not sharp spiritually and having difficulty understanding biblical concepts. This dullness is not caused by outside forces but by their own choices, lethargy, and laziness. Instead of progressing spiritually, they had either stayed at the same level or regressed.

**Key Biblical Cross-References:**
- Deuteronomy 6:7: Teaching children God's commands throughout daily life
- Colossians 3:16: Teaching and admonishing one another with wisdom
- Matthew 28:20: Teaching others to obey Christ's commands
- Mark 5:19: Telling others what the Lord has done

### The Expectation of Teaching

**"By this time you ought to be teachers"** - After sufficient time exposed to biblical teaching, every believer should become a teacher in some capacity. This doesn't require the spiritual gift of teaching; it's a natural progression for all believers to pass on what they've learned.

The model follows these principles:
- Reproduction (2 Timothy 2:2): Training people to train others to train others
- Jesus' apprenticeship model: Teaching disciples who would teach others
- Every believer should be HOT: Hearing, Obeying, and Telling

**Instead of growth**, these believers needed constant reviewing and reminding of basic principles, like a piano student who never practices and doesn't remember previous lessons.

### Milk vs. Solid Food Analogy

**Spiritual babies** are normal initially, but remaining a baby forever is abnormal - like a spiritual disease. Just as physical babies are expected to grow, spiritual growth to maturity is God's expectation for all believers.

**Solid food represents:**
- Obeying known principles while digging deeper into God's Word
- Understanding more of God's character
- Applying biblical principles to every aspect of life
- Advanced applications beyond basic salvation concepts

### Training for Spiritual Discernment

**"Practice makes perfect"** - We must train ourselves to chew the meat of God's Word. Like learning to chew physical food, spiritual discernment requires practice until it becomes second nature.

**Methods for spiritual growth include:**
- Listening to sermons
- Reading Christian books
- Meditating on Scripture
- Using five Bible study methods: hear, read, study, memorize, meditate
- Challenging ourselves mentally with difficult passages

**The goal: Discerning good and evil** - This isn't merely intellectual but practical wisdom for making righteous decisions. Discernment means seeing into the heart of matters, discovering truth, and acting on that truth.

## Study Questions and Applications

### For Christ as High Priest:
- How did Christ's suffering/crying/tears help Him fulfill requirements of being high priest?
- How did Christ "learn obedience" while remaining perfect?
- How do Christ's earthly experiences perfect Him as our high priest?

### For Spiritual Maturity:
- Are you growing in Christ as much as you'd like?
- What causes you to get "stuck" at certain spiritual levels?
- How can you partake of solid food instead of milk?
- What can you do to "chew" on the meat of God's Word?
- Are you teaching others what you've learned about Christ?

## Key Takeaways

1. **Christ's humanity qualifies Him as our high priest** - His suffering and temptation enable Him to sympathize with our weaknesses while remaining sinless.

2. **Spiritual maturity is expected, not optional** - Believers should progress from receiving teaching to teaching others, following the model of reproduction and apprenticeship.

3. **Growth requires intentional practice** - Like physical development, spiritual discernment improves through consistent engagement with God's Word and application of biblical principles.

4. **The goal is practical righteousness** - Spiritual maturity enables believers to discern good and evil, making wise decisions that honor God in every aspect of life.

This study emphasizes both the theological foundation of Christ's priesthood and the practical necessity of spiritual growth, challenging believers to move beyond spiritual infancy toward mature faith that impacts others.
