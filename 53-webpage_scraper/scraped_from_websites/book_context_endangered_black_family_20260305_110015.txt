**The Endangered Black Family** has been praised as "one of the most important analyses to appear in the last fifteen years" by Haki Madhubuti, Poet, Publisher, and Educator. The book offers novel insights into black male/female relations, black family destruction, and black male suppression, particularly examining the period from 1969 to 1984 when black males were marginalized.

Called prescient for its foresight, this work represents the final compendium of fifteen years of relentless investigation by a husband-wife team. Written by the founding editors of the journal "Black Male/Female Relationships," it is presented in accessible language while maintaining documentary authority.

The book is widely regarded as the most insightful and courageous analysis of black male/female conflict ever written. It provides an account of how the family and reproductive agendas of a white-dominated society contributed to the decay of the black family and family relationships, offering comprehensive insights into these critical social dynamics.

**The Endangered Black Family: Coping With the Unisexualization and Coming Extinction of the Black Race** by Dr. Nathan Hare and Julia Hare (1984) analyzes the breakdown of the Black family unit, attributing it to legacies of slavery, racism, and "unisexualization" - the blurring of traditional gender roles between men and women.

**Key Themes:**

**The "Unisexualization" Argument:** The Hares argue that the women's liberation movement, combined with societal pressures, forced Black women into roles that blurred gender lines, causing conflict and instability in relationships.

**Historical Impact:** Historical factors starting from slavery and continuing through racism created an "endangered" status for the traditional Black family structure.

**Critical Analysis:** The book provides critical analysis of how socioeconomic factors and shifting gender dynamics have contributed to challenges facing the Black family.

**Reception:** Reviews are mixed - some readers praise it as crucial, conscious, and "fabulous" work, while others found arguments difficult to follow or heavily supported by the authors' own research rather than external sources.

**Relevance:** Remains a notable text in discussions about sociology, gender, and the Black family, often used in studies focusing on the history of Black women and relationships. Considered a deeply conscious, often provocative examination of Black relationships.
